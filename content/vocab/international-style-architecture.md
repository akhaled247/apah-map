International Style architecture is a major modernist movement that emerged in Europe in the 1920s and dominated global building design through the 1970s. It is defined by simple geometric forms, an absence of applied decoration, and the prominent use of industrial materials like glass, steel, and reinforced concrete.

The International Style is a highly specific subset of modernism defined by an absolute lack of applied decoration, an emphasis on volume over mass, and strict geometric regularity.
