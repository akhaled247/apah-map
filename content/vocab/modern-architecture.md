Modern architecture is a 20th-century design movement characterized by minimalism, the rejection of historical ornamentation, and the principle that "form follows function". It embraced new industrial materials like steel, concrete, and plate glass. Famous pioneers include Le Corbusier and Frank Lloyd Wright.

Modern architecture is a broad philosophical movement rejecting historical ornamentation in favor of new materials and functionalism. The International Style is a highly specific subset of modernism defined by an absolute lack of applied decoration, an emphasis on volume over mass, and strict geometric regularity.
