# 28 — Peplos Kore from the Acropolis

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Archaic Greek. 
**Date:** c. 530 BCE
**Medium:** Marble, painted details
**Location:** Athens Acropolis, Greece

## Form

- The sculpture is just under 4’ in height made from parian marble.
- Parian marble is a fine-grained semi translucent pure-white and entirely flawless marble quarried during the classical era on the Greek island of Paros in the Aegean Sea.
- The figure wears a peplos, and underneath a thin chiton (Kii-ton) which peeps out from the sleeves and hem.
- Peplos is a garment worn in ancient Greece, full length and tied at the waist.
- She was probably decorated with bronze head decorations and shoulder fibulae.
- Fibulae is a device to hold a garment together; similar to a pin, brooch, etc
- There are holes on her head that suggest she could have been fitted with a crown.
- She displays an Archaic smile, which is an expression that is characteristic of early Greek sculpture. It shows a sense of well being.
- The left arm, which is now missing, was made of a separate piece of stone.
- Encaustic paint \= pigments with hot wax burned in an inlay
- Originally painted in blue, red, yellow

## Function

- While Korai figures were generally offerings to Athena, this specific one is clothed in a way which is not replicated in the other korai figures in the Acropolis.
- Normally, kore statues are commissioned to serve as offerings to worship deities, as votive figures who stand in their place.
- Richer individuals used the kore statues as grave markers for deceased family members.

## Content

- A kore is a standing Archaic stone statue (typically in marble or limestone) of a draped, unmarried female figure. Usually these statues were life-size.
- The function of this piece is suggested to be a representation of either Artemis or Athena, both Greek goddesses.
- This kore wears an unusual costume that sets her apart from other korai, but because her arms do not survive, we can’t tell whether she held the goddess’s attributes.

## Context

- Kore is the female version of Kouros (which is the male figure)
- Korai is plural
- Ancient Greece worshiped and constructed statues of gods and goddesses who they believed influenced mortal lives such as Zeus, Hera, and in this case, either Artemis or Athena.
- Peplos Kore was dedicated to her, and was placed in the Acropolis in Athens.
- The red garment that she wears, called a peplos, is a rectangle of cloth, often linen, that is pinned at the shoulders and then falls down.
- Korai (female clothed figures) and Kouroi (male nude figures) were both found in great numbers during the archaic period.
- The kore was found in pieces in the “Korai Pit” near the Erechtheion
- The Peplos Kore, along with fourteen other korai were discovered dumped in the pit on the Acropolis
- During renovations following the Persian destruction of the Acropolis between 480-479 BCE., the Athenians ensured these broken and damaged dedications remained sacred to Athena by burying them on the Acropolis.
