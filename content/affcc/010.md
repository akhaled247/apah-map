# 10 — Tlatilco female figurine

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Tlatilco culture. 
**Date:** 1200–900 BCE
**Medium:** Ceramic with traces of pigment
**Location:** Tlatilco (Central Mexico)

## Form

- Two conjoined heads
- Line of symmetry running vertically through the form.
- Unnatural proportions of arms, legs, torso
- Narrow torso
- Wide hips and thighs
- Truncated feet and hands
- Muted colors, remnants of pigment
- Texture contrasts -
- Rows of hash marks within the hair
- Smoother texture over most of the form
- Roundness of form is emphasized, especially in the legs
- Small in form - approximately 3.75” in height
- Pinch construction

## Function

- Lack of written record makes function/meaning difficult to ascertain
- These types of objects were found buried in graves, maybe a funerary purpose?

## Content

- This particular work is a Bicephalic (two headed) woman
- Men figurines are rare, with many of them wearing ritualistic attire

## Context

- Came from the tegion of the Aztecs,  however it predates Aztec society
- Many small figurines are found in this area, most are female
- People of Tlatico were farmers, with maize being the main crop
- Villages were near a lake, and the site of modern day Mexico City
- Objects were found in large quantity by brick makers who sold the items
