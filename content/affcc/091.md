# 91 — Las Meninas

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Diego Velázquez. 
**Date:** 1656 CE
**Medium:** Oil on canvas
**Location:** Madrid, Spain

## Form

- Large oil painting - 125 1/4 x 108 5/8 in.
- Low key
- View of artist’s studio, paintings in background as well as the large canvas being executed
- Space is important - back, middle and foreground

## Function

- Possibly to elevate the role of the artist; less of a painting about the girl, but more about the artist painting the girl.
- This painting hung in King Philip IV’s study (father of the girl)

## Content

- Small girl, her attendants, the artist, the king and queen possibly reflected in a mirror
  - Infanta Margarita of Spain was the daughter of King Philip IV and his second wife, Queen Mariana of Austria.
- Velazquez wears the cross of the Royal Order of Santiago, which elevates him to knighthood.

## Context

- Velazquez Bio
  - Although his early paintings were religious-themed, he became renowned for his realistic, complex portraits as a member of King Philip IV's court. In his later years, the Spanish master produced a renowned portrait of Pope Innocent X and the famed "Las Meninas."
  - became influenced by the naturalism of Italian painter Caravaggio
  - Had contact with  Peter Paul Reubens as well as travels to Italy which also influenced his work.
