# 47 — Ludovisi Battle Sarcophagus

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Late Imperial Roman. 
**Date:** c. 250 CE
**Medium:** Marble
**Location:** Rome, Italy

## Form

- Large block of marble, heavily carved in high relief. The approximate height is 5’
- Entire surface is covered
- Even though figures overlap, the picture plane is flattened, with all the action coming forward.
- Romans figures are idealized, appearing noble while Goths look cartoon-like.
  - Hair, beard, exaggerated noses.
  - Wild expression of the Goths
- Its use of space is an indication of Late Roman artists’ rejection of Classical perspective.
- The central figure becomes a focal in a chaotic scene by
  - His placement toward the horizontal center
  - Gesturing hand and lack of helmet, expression of confidence and calm
  - Implied lines leading to his form
    - Cape
    - Within the forms of the scene there are vertical, horizontal and diagonals that lead the eye.

## Function

- Funerary - this is a sarcophagus (a stone coffin)

## Content

- Battle between the Romans and the Barbarians (probably the Goths)

## Context

- Burials, as opposed to cremation,  were becoming increasingly popular and sarcophagi were in greater demand.
- The identity of the individual for whom this was made is unknown, but due to the size of the marble, and the time/effort/cost required to carve this marble, it was certainly someone of importance and wealth.
- This work marks a definite shift away from the classical Greek carvings. This is a change of style between high classical work and the late Imperial period.
- The Roman empire wasn’t as stable as it was during the time of Augustus. There is war, including civil war and internal struggle for power with the assassination of various emperors.
  - This work could have been meant to paint a picture of Rome that is more staunch than it really was.
