# 8 — Stonehenge

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Neolithic Europe. 
**Date:** c. 2500–1600 BCE
**Medium:** Sandstone (sarsen and bluestone)
**Location:** Wiltshire, United Kingdom

## Form

- Large ditch (henge) approximately 360 feet in diameter
- pits slightly more than 3’ in diameter
- There were three stages of development. Upright wooden posts were added in the center and at the two entrances during stage 2
- Stones
- Blue stones (volcanic and igneous rocks) placed in Aubrey holes
- Sarsen stones (silicified sandstone) placed in 108’ diameter circle
- Post and lintel construction
- Tongue and groove
- Mortise and tenon joints

## Function

- After the second stage of construction, it was used, at least in part for burials. Cremated remains are still buried in the Aubrey holes
- Would appear to have a ritual significance as the axis of the structure is aligned with the sunrise of the summer solstice and the setting of the midwinter sun
- Recent research suggests that is was possibly a place for healing of the sick and a pilgrimage site

## Context

- One of the most recognizable monuments from the neolithic period
- Located on the Salisbury plain in England
- Built in at least three phases of construction
- Stage 1 ca 3100 bce - henge (the ditch)
  - The oldest part of the Stonehenge monument was built during the period from 3000 to 2935 BCE. It consists of a circular enclosure that is more than 330 feet (100 metres) in diameter, enclosing 56 pits called the Aubrey Holes, named after John Aubrey, who identified them in 1666. The ditch of the enclosure is flanked on the inside by a high bank and on the outside by a low bank
- Stage 2 was approximately 100-200 years later
  - About 2500 BCE the sarsen stones were brought from the Avebury area of the Marlborough Downs
  - They were then arranged inside the circle in a horseshoe-shaped setting of five tall trilithons (paired uprights with a lintel)—the central and largest of which is known as the giant trilithon—surrounded by 30 uprights linked by curved lintels to form a circle.
- Stage 3 was long lasting and began roughly 400-500 years later
  - side ditches and banks of a ceremonial avenue almost 2 miles (3 km) long were dug from Stonehenge to the River Avon at some time in the period between 2470 and 2280 BCE.
- Likely built by early farmers of nearby settlements
- Equinox and Solstices
  - Equinox - night  and day are approximately equal
  - Solstices - longest and shortest days of the year, depending on season
- Bluestones
  - quarried from the Preseli Hills 160 miles away
  - They weigh between 2 and 5 tons each
- Sarsens
  - On average the sarsens weigh 25 tons
  - Quarried at Marlborough Downs, 20 miles away
- Conservation
  - Has undergone restoration and conservation efforts
    - 1919, 1958 and 1959, 1964 and 2021
- The site is still a sacred place of special religious and cultural significance for many
