# 94 — Screen with the Siege of Belgrade and hunting scene

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Circle of the González Family. 
**Date:** c. 1697–1701 CE
**Medium:** Tempera and resin on wood, shell inlay (enconchado)
**Location:** Mexico City, Mexico

## Form

- Biombos and enconchados
  - Biombos: a folding screen
  - Enconchados: mother of pearl inlaid paintings
- The work originally included six additional panels, which are now in Mexico.
- Dark borders with Japanese floral motifs
- Belgrade side - Habsburg empire at war with the Spanish Turks
  - Picture Plane is packed with imagery
- Hunting scene is more of a decorative landscape
  - Copy of a French made, Medici tapestry
- European, classicising motifs on both sides

## Function

- Each side was meant to be seen by different people
  - Battle side: viceroy and important individuals who would have visited
  - Shows representation of of power
- Hunting scene was more likely to be seen by the Viceroy’s wife and her female guests

## Content

- Hunting scene on one side and Siege of Belgrade on the other

## Context

- Commissioned in Mexico City by the viceroy of New Spain, it was most likely displayed in Mexico’s viceregal palace, where it would have divided a ceremonial state room from a more intimate sitting room.
- The work is inspired by Japanese folding screens
- Habsburg family ruled Spain, much of the new world and much of central Europe
- The work was created shortly after the actual event
- Stylistically a mix of thin painting, use of line and contour and mixed with shell.
- The artist is unknown, but it was made locally for the viceroyalty
- The images on both sides of the screen were copied from prints coming from Europe.
- The wife of the Viceroy was a descendant of Moctezuma, thus even though conquered, important individuals of the indigenous peoples still held a degree of importance
