# 72 — Birth of Venus

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Sandro Botticelli. 
**Date:** c. 1484–1486 CE
**Medium:** Tempera on canvas
**Location:** Florence, Italy

## Form

- Renaissance
- Showing depth, but at the same time flattening through the use of patterning
  - Flowers
  - Clothing
  - Seashell and waves
- Darker values advance, lighter receding
- Figures on a similar plane/distance from viewer
- Expressive position of form/line
- inspiration from statues of the classical era for the modest attitude of Venus

## Function

- Uncertain, perhaps at a wedding celebration?

## Content

- Pagan subject - Venus Goddess of Love
- Seashell/water - Venus born from the sea
- Born fully grown
- Celebration of beauty and love
- Location is a landing on the island of Cyprus
- young woman welcomes her, sometimes identified with one of the Graces or with the Hour of Spring
- Representation of orange trees, considered a Medici emblem due to the similarity between the family name and the one with which these plants were known, 'mala medica

## Context

- Today it is in the Uffizi gallery
- Not sure of the patron, painting was at some point located in a Medici villa.
- Don’t know the intended location
- Full length nudes unusual for 15th century subject matter
  - Did appear in art during Medieval and Renaissance periods, but usually Adam and Eve
  - Usually represented religious subjects (consider Donatello’s David)
