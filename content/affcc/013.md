# 13 — Palette of King Narmer

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Predynastic Egypt. 
**Date:** c. 3000–2920 BCE
**Medium:** Greywacke
**Location:** Hierakonpolis, Egypt

## Form

- ” in height
- Contains 3 registers on the back and 4 on the front
- Central placement of Narmer
- Hierarchical scale
- Composite views typical of Egyptian art (all the body's features but the torso are shown in profile)
- Line of symmetry runs vertically on both sides

## Function

- Ceremonial palette used in the ritual mixing and application of the King’s eye makeup
- Gives Narmer the status of being equal to a god

## Content

- Depicts the unification of upper and lower Egypt
- Back registers
- Narmer wearing crown of Upper Egypt, while killing or possibly sacrificing and enemy with a mace (club)
- Two enemy dead in the lower register
- Upper register has two images of Hathor looking down. Narmer’s name appears between the two images of Hathor
- Falcon representing god Horus
- Man’s head emerges from papyrus flowers (papyrus flowers represent Lower Egypt
- The servant on left holds Narmer’s sandals
- Front registers
- Top register is repeated
- Second register
- Victory in battle with Narmer wearing the red crown of Lower Egypt
- Bodies of enemies with heads decapitated
- Third register shows Narmer’s servants controlling 2 lionesses with leashes.
- Narmer controls servants who in turn control the cats.
- This declares the king’s authority
- Bottom register shows Narmer as a bull. (Egyptian gods could change shapes). He is crushing a rebellious town.

## Context

- This object was found ritually buried with other items that had been used for ceremonial purposes
- Items ritually buried were offerings to gods. It was meant to demonstrate piety on the part of the donor and improve standing with the god.
- Upper and Lower Egypt
- Lower Egypt is to the north
- Upper is to the south.
- Names reflect the flow of the Nile River, from South to North.
- Prior to unification, most Egyptians lived in one of two different kingdoms. They shared some cultural norms, but were under different rulers.
- Symbols of Egypt:
- Uraeus/Cobra symbolized Lower Egypt while a Vulture represented Upper Egypt. Both were symbols of protection.
- Plants: Lotus symbolized Upper Egypt; Papyrus, Lower Egypt.
