# 43 — Augustus of Prima Porta

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Imperial Roman. 
**Date:** Early first century CE
**Medium:** Marble (copy of bronze original)
**Location:** Prima Porta (Villa of Livia), Rome, Italy

## Form

- Life size with total height a little under 7’
  - Standing contrapposto
  - Presented in the pose of an orator
    - Right arm raised, left bent at the elbow and supporting a drapery
  - Uses naturalism in the pose and sense of movement, however it shows a youthful, athletic Augustus which is similar to Classical Greek sculptures and contrasts the Republican Rome veristic portraits.

## Function

- Used as propaganda piece

## Content

- The sculpture is full of military and religious symbolism
- Cupid riding a dolphin
  - Reference to a naval victory over Mark Anthony and Cleopatra, making Augustus the sole ruler over the Roman Empire
- Breastplate
  - Symbols signifying military victory
  - His rule being divinely sanctioned by representation of gods
  - Signified Pax Romana, a time of peace
- A claim to be a descendant of a goddess, thus equating himself with a deity
- The similarity of the pose with the Doryphoros would appear to make a reference to the Golden Age of Greece; indicating a Golden Age of Rome

## Context

- The name comes from the city where it was found
- This was possibly a marble copy of an original bronze (no longer in existence). So the bronze was meant for public viewing and the marble meant for private.
- Found on the estate of Livia, Augustus’ wife
