# 65 — Alhambra

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Nasrid Dynasty. 
**Date:** 1354–1391 CE
**Medium:** Whitewashed adobe stucco, wood, tile, paint, and gilding
**Location:** Granada, Spain

## Form

- (1 mile) of walls with thirty towers of varying size enclose this city within a city.
- four main gates
- Placed on a high hilltop (good location for defensive purposes)
- Contained a residence for the ruler and close family, the citadel, barracks for the elite guard who were responsible for the safety of the complex, and an area called medina (or city), near the Puerta del Vino (Wine Gate), where court officials lived and worked.
- highly ornate interior spaces and patios contrast with the plain walls of the fortress exterior
- Three palaces: Comares Palace, the Palace of the Lions, and the Partal Palace, each of which was built during the 14th century.
- Has several pools and gardens
  - A fourth was later begun by a Christian ruler Carlos V.
- Whitewashed brick, made from the red soil of the region. As the whitewash has faded, color of the brick has resulted in a reddish appearance
- complex geometric tile and carved stucco panels

## Function

- Served as a palace and a fortress
- residence for the ruler and close family
- the citadel
- Alcazaba— fortification with barracks for the elite guard who were responsible for the safety of the complex
- an area called medina (or city), near the Puerta del Vino (Wine Gate), where court officials lived and worked

## Context

- It was built by the Nasrid Dynasty (1232-1492)—the last Muslims to rule in Spain.
- The name means red fort
- Alhambra is distinctive among Medieval palaces for its sophisticated plan, complex decoration, and its many gardens and fountains.
- Alhambra's architecture shares many characteristics with other examples of Islamic architecture, but has a unique relationship between interior and exterior spaces.
- A large fourth palace was later begun by the Christian ruler, Carlos V.
- The fortifications date back to the 9th century, but it didn’t become a residence of kings until the 13th century
- The Alhambra became a Christian court in 1492 when the Catholic Monarchs conquered Granada.
