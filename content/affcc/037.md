# 37 — Winged Victory of Samothrace

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Hellenistic Greek. 
**Date:** c. 190 BCE
**Medium:** Marble
**Location:** Samothrace, Greece

## Form

- The figure is carved marble and attached to a base, which is a marble carved ship’s bow. The two marbles contrast each other in color and value.
- She has obviously lost her head and both arms. Even the right wing is a re-creation.
- Traces of blue paint have been found on the wing.
- Wind blown dress that clings, revealing the form of the body underneath.
- The illusion of flowing fabric helps to communicate the perception of motion. It also provides a variety of angles which adds energy.

## Function

- The theory is that it was made to commemorate a sea battle.

## Content

- The subject is Nike, Winged goddess of victory

## Context

- Originally located in the Sanctuary of the Great Gods on the isle of Samothrace
- She was set in the war gallery as part of a two tiered fountain. Large rocks filled the lower section and the figure was at the top.  Water flowed around the sculpture giving the impression of rushing waves.
- At one time, it was believed that she might have held a trumpet in her hands. After the location of a hand in 1950, it suggests that she was making a gesture of greeting.
- The forms and subjects of the Hellenistic period departed from the Polykleitos ideal form.
- Winged Victory of Samothrace is one of the best known examples of Hellenistic art.
- Currently resides in the Louvre in France
- Discovered in pieces by Charles Champoiseau, an amateur archeologist who found it in 1866
- At the time, travelers and archeologists would search excavation sites in hopes of finding items to collect in homes and museums. People saw these items as a sign of status.
- Once again, this type of collecting raises ethical questions.
