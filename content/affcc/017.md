# 17 — Great Pyramids (Menkaura, Khafre, Khufu) and Great Sphinx

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Old Kingdom, 4th Dynasty, Egypt. 
**Date:** c. 2550–2490 BCE
**Medium:** Cut limestone
**Location:** Giza, Egypt

## Form

- Great Pyramids and Sphinx are part of a necropolis complex
- The complex also contains smaller pyramids for the queens and mastabas for other elites as well as lesser cemeteries
- There is a valley temple to the east of each pyramid, connected by a long causeway to a funerary temple at the base of the pyramid
- Pyramids are in alignment

## Function

- Funerary, burial of the pharaohs

## Content

- Items that the pharaoh would need in the afterlife were placed in the pyramid
- The shape was a possible reference to the sun, sides representing the rays pointing upward, but also a ramp upward for the pharaoh.

## Context

- The location of the necropolis complex where the pyramids reside is near the west banks of the Nile river, at the edge of modern day Cairo
- The largest and oldest of the 3 was built for Khufu, next was Kahfre and then the smallest, for Menkaura
- After their construction, the pyramids remained the tallest structures for over 3 thousand years.
- Construction is thought to have skilled labor and possibly slave labor
- Speculation exists over how the stones were transported an lifted into position
- There has been a discovery of a town for workers south of the complex
- Egypt is not the only place with pyramids. Other African countries as well as Mesopotamia have pyramids or step pyramids. There was an obvious exchange of ideas from travel and trade.
- With their ascension to the throne, pharaohs took on a near divine nature. The “ka” or spirit of kingship was often depicted next to the pharaoh. The god Horus (the falcon) was associated with the living king.
- In modern times, urban encroachment has made its way up to the complex almost up to the sphinx. With it, problems of pollution, waste, illegal activity, auto traffic threaten the preservation of the site.
