# 40 — Alexander Mosaic from the House of the Faun, Pompeii

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Republican Roman copy of c. 315 BCE Greek wall painting by Philoxenos of Eretria. 
**Date:** c. 100 BCE
**Medium:** Mosaic
**Location:** Pompeii, Italy

## Form

- Size is 8’11” x 16’8”
- Mosaic composed of tiny tesserae (tiny pieces of glass)
  - Small shapes allowed for more detail
  - It is estimated that there are around 1.5 million pieces of stone and glass.
- Colors used are predominantly warm earth tones (ochres and sienna hues)
- Crowded shallow space filled with action/movement
- Expressive faces and gestures
- Repetition of direction - angle of the spears and both horses and human forms.
- Great detail and modeling of forms
- There is negative space at the top of the composition with most of the action taking place in the lower ⅔’s of the picture plane.
- There is extensive use of foreshortening

## Function

- It was a piece of art that aristocrats would have invested in for perhaps, their enjoyment and for the enjoyment of guests

## Content

- Commemorated a battle of Issus (333 BCE), between Alexander the Great and King Darius III of Persia.
  - A battle in which Alexander the Great was victorious.

## Context

- The work was originally a floor mosaic in the House of Faun in Pompeii. It was a room that was between two peristyle gardens.
- This was protected and remains in reasonable condition due to a layer of ash from the Mt. Vesuvius of 79 CE
- This artwork is believed to have been a copy of a Greek painting. We don’t typically think about Greeks painting, as most of it doesn’t survive.
- Much of what we know about Greek paintings comes from writings and Roman copies.
- During this time period, Greek things were in great demand in Pompeii. People wanted to identify with the ancient Greeks. Consequently, it was very common to find Roman copies of Greek artworks.
