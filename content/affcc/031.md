# 31 — Temple of Minerva (Veii, near Rome, Italy) and sculpture of Apollo

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Master sculptor Vulca (attributed). 
**Date:** c. 510–500 BCE
**Medium:** Original temple of wood, mud brick, or tufa; terra cotta sculpture
**Location:** Veii (near Rome, Italy)

## Form

- Near square tufa block foundation
- There is a deep porch with 8 Etruscan style columns (2 rows of 4), occupying approximately ½ of the structure and the remaining enclosure divided into three rooms (a triple cella)
- The base is high and there is only one set of steps which are located in the front of the temple
- The roofs were constructed of wood and tile, and decorated by terracotta sculptures
- Columns were wood, unfluted, with tops similar to the Doric order, bottoms were however, set upon bases
- Apollo of Veii
- Animated facial expression
- Gesturing hands
- Left foot forward
- Decorated base support
- attention to detail on garment

## Function

- Religious
- Housing sculptures of goddesses
- Sculptures on the roof provide a narrative

## Content

- (on the roof) Apollo and Hercules in battle over a giant deer sacred to Apollo’s twin sister, Artemis

## Context

- The Etruscan civilization flourished in central Italy between the 8th and 3rd century BCE.
- It was known as a major Mediterranean trading power
- Much of its culture and even history was either obliterated or assimilated into that of its conqueror, Rome.
- With Gaul/Celtic invasions from the north and Roman annexation/conquest from the south, Etruscan civilization ended and was more or less assimilated into Roman culture.
- It was from the Etruscans that the Romans inherited many of their own cultural and artistic traditions
- The Etruscans were, like the Greeks and Romans, polytheists.
- There were some Etruscan counterparts to Greek gods
- Minerva was the goddess of wisdom, war, art, schools, justice and commerce. She was the Etruscan counterpart to Greek Athena.
- Greek temples likely influenced the desire to create temples dedicated to Etruscan gods.
- While influenced by the Greeks, Etruscan temples were different in design and the materials used.
- Stone foundations survive, but other materials used were not as permanent as stone used by Greek builders, therefore little remains of Etruscan temples
- Much of our knowledge of Etruscan temples comes from the writings of the Roman architect, Vitruvius.
