# 29 — Sarcophagus of the Spouses

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Etruscan. 
**Date:** c. 520 BCE
**Medium:** Terra cotta
**Location:** Cerveteri, Italy

## Form

- Husband and wife reclining as if at a banquet
- The figures are stylized, simplified
- There is a relaxed, casual appearance
- Etruscan Characteristics
- Focus on upper half of body
- Gestures of hands
- Animated facial expressions
- Life sized
- Terracotta - low fire clay, not terribly strong/durable
- Cast in 4 pieces
- Originally, it was brightly painted

## Function

- Funerary - a place for the cremated remains of the dead
- Sarcophagus - like a coffin, usually stone, decorated, houses the corpse, sometimes buried, sometimes above ground

## Content

- This was a husband and wife reclining as if they were at a banquet
- The forms are atop a dining couch
- Uncertainty about what figures were holding, maybe food, drink

## Context

- This work would have been created near the height of Etruscan civilization, when its city states controlled what is now central Italy
- Etruscan women had a sense of freedom and an independence that Greek women didn’t enjoy.
- Wives didn’t attend Greek symposiums and recline with their husbands on banquet couches like Etruscan women did.
- Etruscan artworks depict women attending sporting events with men
- Inscriptions reflect higher status of women, stating names of both fathers and mothers of commemorated individuals.
- The figures within this piece reflect the Etruscan view of death, that it was a pathway into eternal life; a time of festivity and celebration as also seen in tomb paintings of the Etruscans
- Contrast the forms
- to Egyptian stiffness; Egyptian forms were frontal, rigid, idealized and formal
- Greek proportions and rendering of form
- This was the late Archaic period, so Greek forms bore similar characteristics to Egyptian stiffness, symmetry and frontal poses
- Nothing similar to this type of sarcophagus found in Greek culture
- Greeks used simple graves with stele or marker
- Greeks graves wouldn’t be large enough to house a large sarcophagus
- Original location: Cerveteri, a town in Italy north of Rome, which is the site of a large Etruscan necropolis
- This work was originally found in hundreds of pieces, requiring reconstruction
