# 57 — Pyxis of al-Mughira

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Umayyad. 
**Date:** 968 CE
**Medium:** Ivory (carved)
**Location:** Madinat al-Zahra, near Córdoba, Spain

## Form

- A little more than 6.25” in height by roughly 4.625 in diameter
- Ivory
- Intricately carved
- Ivory: Ivory was durable, smooth, elegant, and easily carved; easily portable
- Jade and other precious and semi-precious stones were used in the decoration of these boxes
- Decorated with 4 eight lobed medallions

## Function

- Pyxis is a cylindrical box used for cosmetics, perfumes, incense
- Gifts given to both men and women

## Content

- The Pyxis of al-Mughira is decorated with four eight-lobed medallions which are surrounded by figures and animals that include falconers, wrestlers, griffons, peacocks, birds, goats and animals to be hunted. Each medallion has princely iconography.
- A lute player flanked by two figures, one of whom holds the braided scepter and flask of the Umayyads, while the other holds a fan. Presumably the man with the scepter and flask symbolizes the Umayyad Caliph, and the figure with the fan, the Abbasids.
  - Could have been expressing the political authority and legitimacy of Umayyad Caliphs (as opposed to the Abbasid Caliphs, who ruled in Baghdad).
- lions attacking two bulls. As in Arabic poetry, these lions symbolize the victorious (in this case, perhaps the Umayyads).
- men on horseback date-picking. The date-palm, found primarily in the Middle East and North Africa, may allude to the lost lands of the East (the lands under Abbasid control).
- Men collecting eggs from the nests of falcons
  - Symbolizing the legitimacy of the Umayyad rule .

## Context

- Ivory was popular from Early Roman through the Byzantine eras throughout Constantinople, Egypt and Syria but not seen in Spain prior to the Umayyad caliph.
- Gifts to members of the royal family, specifically sons, wives and daughters on important or memorable occasions
- This was a gift to the then-eighteen-year-old al-Mughira, the son of a caliph
  - A caliph is the chief Muslim civil and religious ruler, regarded as the successor of Muhammad.
- This is among the best surviving examples of the royal ivory carving tradition in Al-Andalus (Islamic Spain), the lands on the Iberian Peninsula which were controlled by Muslims from 711 to 1492
- Current location is now in the Louvre in France
