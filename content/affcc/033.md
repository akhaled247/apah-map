# 33 — Niobides Krater

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Anonymous vase painter known as the Niobid Painter. 
**Date:** c. 460–450 BCE
**Medium:** Clay, red-figure technique (white highlights)
**Location:** Orvieto, Italy (Attic origin, Athens)

## Form

- Clay
- Inverted bell shape with foot and two curved handles
- Surface is black with red figures and red patterning above near the rim and below the scene, toward the lower third of the bowl
- Figures are in the red of the clay, while the negative space and details are painted in black slip
- Allows the painter to include fine details.
- Highly stylized, symmetrical and repeating encircled plant designs in the bands at the top and bottom in combination with an egg and dart pattern
- Use of red figure pottery

## Function

- A krater is a type of Greek vase that is a bowl for mixing wine with water
- These vases symbolized wealth, sophistication by owners

## Content

- It is a story from Greek mythology where the gods are quick to take vengeance on human arrogance and pride
- Niobe’s children being killed by Apollo and Artemis

## Context

- The style of handles on this krater curve upward from the “cul” (low part of the bowl) and resemble the calyx of a flower. Therefore this is known as a CALYX KRATER
- These kraters were valuable trade items with people like the Etruscans who put them in their tombs.
- This particular work is not considered to be the best quality by this particular artist. It is because it is the earliest contemporaneous witness to the new developments in mural painting in the Early Classical Period
