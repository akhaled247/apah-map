# 53 — Merovingian looped fibulae

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Early medieval Europe. 
**Date:** Mid-sixth century CE
**Medium:** Silver gilt worked in filigree, with inlays of garnets and other stones
**Location:** Jouy-le-Comte, France

## Form

- Highly ornamental “safety pin”
- Body, pin and a catch
- Material: silver gilt-worked in filigree with inlaid garnet and other stones
- Decorated with garnets, amethyst, and colored glass
- Filigree: ornamental work of fine (typically gold or silver) wire formed into delicate tracery.
- Use of Cloisonne’ - means “partitioned” in French
- Website info on enameling: [http://www.jeantudorenamels.com/artofenameling/artofenameling_pv.html](http://www.jeantudorenamels.com/artofenameling/artofenameling_pv.html)
- Enamel is a colored glass used in a cloisonne’ method that gained widespread popularity in europe during the middle ages
- Semi-precious stones
- Silver Gilt

## Function

- Clasp or safety pin type of device for pinning clothing/garments
- These were expensive objects to the commission: the owners wanted these objects to resonate with their identity
- These items were commonly buried with the dead: showed their status and who they were as people

## Content

- Heads of eagles - sign of Roman empire and strength
- Fish

## Context

- These were found at a Visigothic grave site in Spain
- Fibulae were made popular by the Roman campaigns and were not unique to a particular region, the fibulae shown for AP are Frankish
- Barbarian as used in this context: non-Roman, nomadic, and illiterate groups traveling throughout Europe during the middle ages
- Here is a link with more info than you will ever want to know about the Franks: [https://www.historyfiles.co.uk/KingListsEurope/FranceFranks.htm](https://www.historyfiles.co.uk/KingListsEurope/FranceFranks.htm)
