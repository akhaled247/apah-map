# 95 — The Virgin of Guadalupe (Virgen de Guadalupe)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Miguel González. 
**Date:** c. 1698 CE
**Medium:** Oil on canvas on wood, inlaid with mother-of-pearl (enconchado)
**Location:** Mexico City, Mexico

## Form

- Virgin Mary
- Roundels in each corner tell her story
  - Mary’s appearance to Juan Diego
    - Her image revealed on Juan’s tunic
    - Mix of oil and mother of pearl
    - Angels support each roundel
    - Symmetry - canvas divided into quadrants
    - Wide, ornate and dark colored frame.
      - Inlaid shell and wood
      - Floral design and other images that symbolize Mary
    - bottom/center is the eagle on a cactus identifying a connection between the virgin and New Spain

## Context

- According to the story, Juan Diego encountered the virgin Mary on three different occasions instructing him to tell the local bishop to build a church on the hill in her honor. The bishop didn’t believe it, so on the 4th visit, Juan was told to gather roses from the hill and place them in his cloak. Upon opening his tunic, Castilian roses (non indigenous to that area) spilled out. They left an image of the virgin on his cloak, known today as the Virgin of Guadalupe. A shrine was then built on the hill and a basilica below.
- Devotion to the Virgin of Guadalupe grew, especially within the creole population (pure blooded Spaniards born in the Americas). Creolization of the cult of the Virgin of Guadalupe in the second half of the seventeenth century contributed to a sense of local identity.
- This work is important because of the use of materials - oil and mother of pearl, because of the connection between Mary and New Spain, and the influence of Asian decorative arts
- Known as an enconchado (concha means shell in Spanish)
