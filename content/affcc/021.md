# 21 — Mortuary Temple of Hatshepsut

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Senenmut (architect). 
**Date:** c. 1473–1458 BCE
**Medium:** Sandstone, partially carved into a rock cliff, and red granite
**Location:** Deir el-Bahri, Egypt

## Form

- terraces - lower, middle and upper
- Each terrace has a colonnade
- Building is symmetrical and rhythmic
- Interior contains murals that are carved in relief
- Statues of herself in traditional pharaoh attire
- Geometric structure contrasts the mountain is into which it is attached

## Function

- The temple is dedicated to the sun god, Amun-Re
- Hatshepsut understood the importance of art in communicating a  message of strength

## Content

- Sculptures of herself portraying strength and power
- The 2nd level colonnade depicts Hatshepsut’s birth and trade with the city of Punt
- Hathor Shrine
- Anubis Shrine
- Amun Shrine

## Context

- Hatshepsut was married to Thutmose II. When he dies, his son Thutmose III becomes pharaoh. Since he is just a child, Hatshepsut takes over as regent. She later proclaims herself to be pharaoh.
- She claims to be the daughter of Amun-Re
- Mentuhotep II’s temple was innovative in that it was a temple integrated into the mountain directly behind it
- Hatshepsut admired his temple, but wanted to exceed it
- She has hers built right next to Mentuhotep, just dramatically larger
- She “co-rules” with her son, but is definitely the one holding power
- When Hatshepsut dies, Thutmose III takes over and toward the end of his reign, Thutmose III tries to wipe her out of history and has much of her art destroyed
- Speculation is that he wanted history to show a continuation of Thutmose I - Thutmose II - Thutmose III without interruption
