# 1 — Apollo 11 Stones

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Paleolithic hunter-gatherers. 
**Date:** c. 25,500–25,300 BCE
**Medium:** Charcoal on stone
**Location:** Huns Mountains, Namibia

## Form

- Stone painted with charcoal, ocher and white
- Simplified silhouette of an animal form (unknown) drawn on surface
- Rendered in profile view to clearly represent the animal.
- Small in size 4.3 x 5”

## Function

- Possibly sympathetic magic, allowing control over animals
- Not known for certain

## Content

- Representation of animal form
- Stylized; not natural
- Mystical content?

## Context

- Found in a cave or rock shelter in the Huns mountains in Namibia (in sw Africa) in 1969
- Evidence appears to show that this was the location of an ongoing settlement that had places of ritual significance
- Named after the successful Apollo 11 mission, the first to take man to the moon.
- The stones were discovered by German archeologist W.E. Wendt
- Dated to Upper Paleolithic time period - these are some of the earliest known rock paintings in Africa
- Nomadic peoples - small size allows portability for the mobile hunter gatherers.
- Stone Age artists from paleo through neolithic periods rendered mostly animals, rarely people
