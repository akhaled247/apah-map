# 11 — Terra cotta fragment

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Lapita culture. 
**Date:** 1000 BCE
**Medium:** Terra cotta (incised)
**Location:** Reef Islands, Solomon Islands

## Form

- Red slip earthenware
- Slip is clay particles and pigment suspended in water and applied to the outside of the pot
- Earthenware is a low fire clay, that is slightly porous
- Probably coil construction possibly using a paddle and anvil technique
- Ornate designs/patterns cover the surface and are filled with a white colorant created from coral lime to contrast the surface color of the pot.
- These pieces contain symmetrical and radial designs created from dots, circles and lines/hatching.
- Most of these pots were simple bowls
- Sand (grog) was added to help reduce shrinkage and breaking during the firing process

## Function

- The pots from which these potsherds come were likely used to serve and store food

## Content

- Geometric designs rich in circles and patterning, along with abstract human forms (anthropomorphic)
- Research has shown that the pottery contained patterns that were part of a visual language that the Lapita artisans adhered to.

## Context

- James Cook and other voyages from the Enlightenment period of the 1700’s speculated about how these islands were populated.
- Since this clay is not indigenous to the Solomon Islands, it indicates an easterly migration of people from Taiwan
- From Taiwan to New Guinea, from there eastward to other Pacific islands
- Stratigraphic archeology of the 1950s demonstrated that Lapitas actually came from areas around Taiwan, not New Guinea
