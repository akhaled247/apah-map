# 69 — David

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Donatello. 
**Date:** c. 1440–1460 CE
**Medium:** Bronze
**Location:** Florence, Italy

## Form

- Renaissance
- Bronze sculpture
- Contrapposto
- Strong sense of movement
- Lost wax process

## Content

- David and Goliath story
  - David with Goliath’s sword and head
  - David symbolized the liberty and freedom of Florences’ republican ideals, which were threatened at various points in the fifteenth century by the Medici family and others.
  - Before Donatello’s work, David was typically depicted as a king, given his status in the Old Testament
    - the nude, but he’s also a youth
    - Hat with laurel - Roman victory
    - trying to associate David’s youth with an innocent and virtuous life
  - Could David’s victory have been gained without divine intervention?  Donatello’s work seems to imply that the answer is “no” – the victory was God’s rather than man’s.

## Context

- Donatello created 2 statues of David in his life
- This was commissioned by Cosimo de’Medici for the Palazzo Medici; located during the 1460’s in the Medici garden
- originally placed on top of a pedestal in the center of the courtyard in the Palazzo Medici, so the viewer would be looking up at it from below (unlike the view we typically get of it in photographs).
- First full standing nude sculpture since classical antiquity; roughly 1000 years
- Considered one of the most important works in the history of western art - Christian subject, Classical style - revolutionary for its time.
- Current location is in the Bargello museum in Florence.
- Several Davids were sculpted during the renaissance period
- The differences between the artist's first David sculpture and the second attest to the evolution of Donatello's humanist perspective, which is emblematic of Renaissance enlightenment.
