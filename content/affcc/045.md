# 45 — Forum of Trajan (Forum, Basilica Ulpia, Trajan Markets, Column of Trajan)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Apollodorus of Damascus. 
**Date:** Forum and markets: 106–112 CE; column: 113 CE
**Medium:** Brick and concrete (architecture); marble (column)
**Location:** Rome, Italy

## Form

- The great hall of the market uses concrete and groin vaults to create a large open space, surrounded by shops on the sides.
- The market was rather innovative while the rest of the forum was more or less conventional.
- The basilica served as the law court
- Used columns and a flat coffered ceiling.
- The column had a narrative scene that ran from top to bottom
- The pictorial scene is carved in relief and approximately 623’ in length
- Celebrates Trajan’s victory in Dacia (101–102 and 105–106 C.E.), culminating with the death of the enemy commander, Decebalus (Dies-bal-us)
- The column itself is just under 125’ tall

## Function

- Forums were a public, urban square for civic and ritual business.
- The market was a collection of over 150 stores (similar to the shopping malls of today)
- Court in the basilica
- Tribute to the military victories of Trajan, more specifically his victory over the Dacians.
- The Dacians were the ancient Indo-European inhabitants of the cultural region of Dacia, located in the area near the Carpathian Mountains and west of the Black Sea.
- The forums conveyed political messages such as the strength and stability of the empire to its citizens and foreign visitors.

## Content

- Forum with entry gates and 2 porticos with exedra
- Basilica Ulpia
- Libraries
- Temple of deified Trajan
- Equestrian Statue
- Trajan’s Column -
- Memorial to Trajan
- Tomb of Trajan
- Narrative of military exploits
- Markets

## Context

- Trajan was the emperor at the height of Rome and his military exploits extended the borders to its greatest reaches.
- Trajan was very popular with the people and the Forum of Trajan was the final, and largest, of Rome’s complex of so-called “Imperial fora”
- The basilica was in the Latin West equivalent to a stoa in the Greek East.
- Markets of Trajan made use of Roman concrete and arches.
- The structure helped to support the hill, much of which had been removed to build the forum.
