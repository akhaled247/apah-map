# 66 — Annunciation Triptych (Merode Altarpiece)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Workshop of Robert Campin. 
**Date:** 1427–1432 CE
**Medium:** Oil on wood
**Location:** Tournai, Belgium

## Form

- Oil on panel
- Highly detailed, many small minute details including background details such as birds, horse and rider, activities of the city
- Symbolic imagery, the wealth of detail has meaning
- Sizes
  - Overall (open): 25 3/8 x 46 3/8 in. (64.5 x 117.8 cm)
  - Central panel: 25 1/4 x 24 7/8 in. (64.1 x 63.2 cm)
  - each wing: 25 3/8 x 10 3/4 in. (64.5 x 27.3 cm)
- Even though the story is old, the setting is contemporary, the clothing is contemporary
- The patron is shown on the left panel, his wife likely added later

## Function

- For use in private prayer

## Content

- angel Gabriel is about to tell the Virgin Mary that she will be the mother of Jesus
- golden rays pouring in through the left oculus carry a miniature figure with a cross
- Joseph, who is betrothed to the Virgin, works in his carpenter’s shop, drilling holes in a board.
- The kneeling donor appears to witness the central scene through the open door.
- Patron’s wife kneels behind him, and a town messenger stands at the garden gate
- The walled garden was symbolic in the late medieval period and early renaissance, representing the virginity of Mary.
- Home was contemporary for the day

## Context

- Work is in superb condition
- associated with the Tournai workshop of Robert Campin (ca. 1375–1444)
- Campin hired at least two assistants and this may be the work of one of them
- Painting appears to be done in phases
- Original may have been done on spec, with additions after purchase
- Donor’s wife may have been painted in after marriage
- The amount of detail is consistent with work from the Northern Renaissance.
- Linear perspective was being developed at this time and not as understood by Renaissance artists in the north as they were in Italy
