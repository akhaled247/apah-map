# 87 — Self-Portrait with Saskia

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Rembrandt van Rijn. 
**Date:** 1636 CE
**Medium:** Etching
**Location:** Amsterdam, Netherlands

## Form

- Dutch Baroque
- Etching (print on paper)
- Dimensions: 4 1/8 × 3 3/4 in.
- Achromatic
- Two figures, filling the majority of the picture plane
- Rembrandt’s form has darker values with aid in creating emphasis and illusion of depth; Saskia’s portrait recedes with the softer mid-tone (using value to create the illusion of depth)

## Function

- Small scale and looseness of the work would indicate that this was either experimental or just enjoying himself with a sketch or doodle.

## Content

- Rembrandt and his wife, both in costume challenging conventional portraiture which typically featured contemporary clothing
- The only portrait of them together, though Saska modeled for her husband often.
- Shows Rembrandt at work

## Context

- Rembrandt is regarded as the greatest practitioner of etching in the history of art and the first to popularize this technique as a major form of artistic expression.
- Rembrandt created some 300 etchings and drypoints from about 1626 to 1665. His career as a printmaker ran parallel to his career as a painter—he rarely treated the same themes in both media and only occasionally did he reproduce his paintings in prints.
  - Drypoint
    - scratches into plate with needle
    - Leaves a slight burr
    - drypoint lines are softer and have blurry edges
    - Won’t get as many prints with the plate as an etching
  - Etching
    - Image drawn into a coated plate, then placed into an acid bath, which etches the image into the plate.
    - Longer plate life means more prints can be made
    - etched lines are more crisp and often have a slight wobble because of how the image is drawn onto the plate
