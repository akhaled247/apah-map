# 42 — Head of a Roman patrician

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Republican Roman. 
**Date:** c. 75–50 BCE
**Medium:** Marble
**Location:** Otricoli, Italy

## Form

- Veristic style portrait
  - Hyper-realistic style
  - Old, wrinkled
  - Exaggerated features

## Function

- Used to help affirm one’s position in society
- These portraits associated the subject with notable Roman Republic ideas.
- Made the subject appear wise and experienced

## Content

- Old man, his identity is unknown

## Context

- Created in later Roman Republic period
- Older men from aristocratic families ruled Rome via the Senate during the 1st century BCE.
- Aristocratic families with a long lineage, would have displayed sculpted busts of their ancestors.
- Veristic portraits were almost always men
- Portraits were in no way idealized, if anything, patricians were made to look older and more serious, virtuous and distinguished than they were.
- With the rise of Rome, there were many newly wealthy individuals without an impressive lineage. For them, veristic portraits were used to enhance their importance.
