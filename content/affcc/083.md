# 83 — Hunters in the Snow

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Pieter Bruegel the Elder. 
**Date:** 1565 CE
**Medium:** Oil on wood
**Location:** Antwerp/Brussels, Belgium

## Form

- Wood panel oil painting
- Muted color, limited palette
- Sense of depth through diminishing scale and aerial perspective
- Strong lines move the viewer through the composition, almost a zig-zag pattern
- Viewer is high within the picture plane
- Demonstrates a foreground, middle and background

## Function

- is part of a long tradition in Northern European art of portraying the months of the year and the activities that occurred during each month.

## Content

- Scenes from everyday life
- Hunters didn’t appear to have a very successful day
- People stoking a fire in far left
- Skaters in middle to background

## Context

- the paintings were commissioned by a wealthy Antwerp banker named Niclaes Jongelinck. Rather than twelve paintings, Bruegel’s cycle divided the year into six seasons (paintings for five survive—the other four are The Harvesters, Return of the Herd, The Gloomy Day, and Haymaking).
- The landscape is given as much emphasis as the activities shown
