# 39 — House of the Vettii

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Imperial Roman (Second and Fourth Styles). 
**Date:** c. second century BCE; rebuilt 62–79 CE
**Medium:** Cut stone and fresco
**Location:** Pompeii, Italy

## Form

- Entrance (fauces)
- An atrium
- The atrium is flanked by wings (alae) and often bounded by bedrooms (cubicula) or shops (tabernae) that were rented out.
- A tablinum, or office for the head of the house, is missing from this plan, but typically linked the public portion of the house to the private courtyard
- A colonnaded courtyard
  - Columns surrounding were of the Doric order and known as a peristyle
  - The center was open to the sky
    - Allow for light
      - Smoke to escape
      - Rain to pass through
    - A small pool or an artificial reservoir used for swimming or as a fish pond or other water collection.
    - Center of family living
- Kitchen
- Bedrooms (cubiculum)
- Frescos (Roman Wall Painting) adorned walls
  - Possibly a transitional period between the third (ornamental) and fourth (intricate) period styles

## Function

- House
- Place to conduct business/entertain guests
- The house played a key part in the reinforcement of social hierarchy, as the patron received clients in the atrium.

## Content

- Gives a glimpse into the rituals of daily life and social hierarchy
- decorations belong to the final period of Pompeii’s occupation
- strongboxes (arca - essentially lockable boxes for storing valuables)—concrete signs of wealth—were placed prominently in the large atrium so that visitors would be sure to notice them
- painting of the god Priapus in the vestibule, serve to underscore the wealth of the brothers Vettii

## Context

- The patron/client relationship was common
  - Patron (well educated, wealthy) would look out for the client
  - Client would support the patron in whatever way needed
- This home belonged to brothers who were former slaves and were now freedmen
- The home was built on the site of another, part of which remains and was incorporated into the existing home.
- It is important both because its large size and wall paintings signify a shift in Roman domus
- Pompeii experienced a significant earthquake in 62 CE
- The earthquake that destroyed the city occurred in 79 CE
  - Being buried in volcanic ash helped preserve much of the house.
