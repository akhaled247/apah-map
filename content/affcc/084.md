# 84 — Mosque of Selim II

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Mimar Sinan (architect). 
**Date:** 1568–1575 CE
**Medium:** Brick, stone, and tile
**Location:** Edirne, Turkey

## Form

- Near square prayer hall, stacked domes
- Minarets
- Large complex
  - Mosque
  - 2 madrasa
  - Shops
- Extensive use of geometric design
- The complex is huge. It measures 623’ x 426’ (or more than the length of two football fields)
- Light, airy feel, with use of blues and contrasting warm rust colors
- Vibrancy created through the use of patterns and repetition
- Piers and squinches support the dome

## Function

- Mosque
- Education
- commercial

## Context

- Commissioned by Sultan Selim II
- Edirne was one of Selim’s favorite cities
- historical and geographic significance
  - Edirne had been a capital of the Ottoman Empire in the 15th century before Istanbul
  - Edirne was the first major city that Europeans traveling to the Ottoman Empire reached - Selim wanted it to be impressive
  - City offered a location and an opportunity to build a mosque that would dominate the city
  - Major city that Europeans would encounter when traveling to the Ottoman Empire.
- Influences
  - The overall plan is unified and open. Thought is given to artful combinations of stacked volumes on the exterior. Unlike many other earlier Ottoman mosques and the early Byzantine church, Hagia Sophia (then converted to a mosque, a museum back to a mosque again), the exterior is clearly not an artistic afterthought but rather an elegant, architectural shell vital to the overall composition.
  - Sinan’s dome, at just over 101’, is larger than Hagia Sophia’s. Obviously a desire to surpass the grandeur of Hagia Sophia.
  - Both HS and Mosque of Selim are located in areas of major trade
  - Both emphasized giant domes with infusion of light
  - HS - is more medieval, built during the early Byzantine era
  - HS, Cordoba were previously churches that became mosques, Selim was built specifically to  be a mosque
