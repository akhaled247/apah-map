# 80 — Venus of Urbino

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Titian. 
**Date:** 1538 CE
**Medium:** Oil on canvas
**Location:** Venice, Italy

## Form

- Oil on canvas, applied in thin glazes
- Horizontal format
- Dimensions: 3′ 11″ x 5′ 5″
- Smooth, sensuous edges, delicately modeled forms, rich color
- Picture plan is divided into halves horizontally, the left side is a shallow space focusing on the form, right side is open, with a view of dog, servants and window
  - Composition is divided in a way that directs the viewer to her genitalia

## Function

- Possible wedding portrait commissioned by the Duke of Urbino
- Some debate that the portrait is courtesan Angela Zaffetta while others have suggested that it is a marriage portrait commissioned by Guidobaldo to celebrate his nuptials to the 10-year-old Giulia Varano in 1534

## Content

- Not sure who the “Venus” is, maybe a prostitute, mistress, or spouse
  - Her gaze is directly into the eyes of the viewer
  - Roses in the right hand
  - Left hand conceals pubic region (similar to Roman sculpture of venus’)
  - Dog on the bed a possible symbol of loyalty, faithfulness

## Context

- Painting was done in a style of the traditional female nude
  - It appears to have been based on Giorgione's Sleeping Venus (1510), although Titian's interpretation of the goddess is much more erotic.
- Titian was a member of the 15th century Venetian School
  - Titian was most famous for his bold utilization of color, particularly in his earlier work, and he achieved this through seeking out rare pigments and using them in their richest and most saturated form
- Canvas was a popular choice in Venice as the damp climate caused warping and splitting of wood panels
