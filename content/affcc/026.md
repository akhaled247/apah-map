# 26 — Athenian Agora

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Archaic through Hellenistic Greek. 
**Date:** 600 BCE–150 CE
**Medium:** Plan / architectural site
**Location:** Athens, Greece

## Form

- Panthiatic way - a road running in a generally north-south direction, it was the path of the annual procession leading up to the Parthenon
- It had such buildings as:
- Altar of the Twelve gods
- Stoa of Zeus, plus 4 additional
- Temple of Apollo Patroos
- A prison
- A library
- A Tholos which served as a dining hall for the senate
- Many additional structures

## Function

- What is an agora?
- This was a place for the people to gather and exchange ideas, debate, conduct trade, etc
- Started as a marketplace and developed into a place of government
- If under attack, it was a place to seek refuge and obtain a strategic position
- A yearly religious procession that was dedicated to Athena would pass through the agora. It would continue up the mountain to the Parthenon.

## Content

- It contained buildings, temples, libraries, law courts
- A Stoa was an especially important structure found in agoras.
- They were covered porticos where people gathered and exchanged ideas, conducted trade, and engaged in various other activities of civic life

## Context

- Agoras began as early as 8th century BCE, in the early Greek archaic period and grew during the classical period.
- Most all the Greek city-states had one
- Agora means “gathering together,” “to assemble,” “marketplace”
- Agoras were frequently located at the base of the sacred spaces. In this case it was the Acropolis
- th century BC, more citizens began to participate in the government
- Direct participation on the part of the people
- The idea of participation based on merit and ability, not wealth, was developed here
- Citizenship required
- Must be male and Athenian
- Both parents must also be Athenian citizens
- There were checks in place to remove individuals who became corrupt
