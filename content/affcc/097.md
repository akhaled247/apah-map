# 97 — Portrait of Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Miguel Cabrera. 
**Date:** c. 1750 CE
**Medium:** Oil on canvas
**Location:** Mexico City, Mexico

## Form

- casta paintings display a mother, father, and a child (sometimes two). This family model is possibly modeled on depictions of the Holy Family showing the Virgin Mary, saint Joseph, and Christ as a child.
- This work is an oil on canvas
- Dimensions: 40” x 49 ⅝”

## Function

- pinturas de castas, or caste paintings
- Casta paintings usually convey the idea that the more European you are, the closer you are to the top of the social and racial hierarchy
- These works usually include a number and text documenting the ethnic mixing that has occurred
  - In this way, casta paintings speak to Enlightenment concerns, specifically the notion that people can be rationally categorized based on their ethnic makeup and appearance.

## Content

- Family - Well dressed indigenous woman, Spanish male, two children. (the older presumably a servant)

## Context

- These works were possibly commissioned by the viceroys
