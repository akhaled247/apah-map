# 59 — Bayeux Tapestry

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Romanesque Europe (English or Norman). 
**Date:** c. 1066–1080 CE
**Medium:** Embroidery on linen
**Location:** Bayeux / Canterbury (Canterbury, England or Normandy, France)

## Form

- Embroidery, not a tapestry
- 229.659’ in length
- The five main colors are blue-green, terracotta, light-green, buff and gray-blue. There are also places where very dark blue, yellow and a dark green have been used. The color of skin has been left as the color of the linen
- The figures are stylized and non-natural
- Latin text is used throughout the work

## Function

- Tells the story of William the Conqueror
- Decor for a new cathedral in Bayeux

## Content

- This is a narrative of the Norman invasion, and subsequent defeat of the Anglo-Saxons at the Battle of Hastings in 1066 CE.
- Gives an authentic view of 11th century life in northern France and England.
- There is a latter section that is missing, probably shows William the Conqueror as king.
- first meal:
  - dining practices
  - examples of armor used
  - battle preparations
  - servants prepare food over a fire and bake bread in an outdoor oven
  - Servants serve the food
  - Bishop Odo, blesses the meal
  - "chickens on skewers, a stew cooked over an open fire and food from an outdoor oven. William sits down to a feast with his nobles and Bishop Odo says grace. Servants load food onto shields to carry it to the banquet"
- cavalry:
  - William's use of cavalry
    - cavalry could advance quickly and easily retreat
    - scattering an opponent's defenses allowing the infantry to invade
  - mainly cavalry meaning that they were most common
  - they wear:
    - conical steel helmets with a protective nose plate
    - mail shirts
    - carry shields and spears
- foot soldiers
  - carrying spears and axes
- horses have no armor
- mortally wounded men and horses along tapestry's mid and bottom
  - "air fills with arrows and lances, men lie dying. The English soldiers, who are all on foot, protect themselves with a wall of shields. The Normans attack from both sides. The lower border of the tapestry is filled with dead and injured soldiers"

## Context

- According to the Bayeux Museum website, the Bayeux Tapestry was likely commissioned to decorate the new cathedral of Bayeux in the 11th century by Bishop Odo - William the Conqueror’s half-brother.
- This was created within one generation of the war, therefore is believed to be a fairly accurate account of events.
- The story of William and the Norman Invasion can be found [here](https://www.bayeuxmuseum.com/en/the-bayeux-tapestry/discover-the-bayeux-tapestry/what-is-the-bayeux-tapestry-about/).
  - 1064 King of England, Edward the Confessor sends his brother-in-law Harold Godwinson to Normandy to offer succession to the throne of England to EC’s cousin William Duke of Normandy.
  - Instead, upon the death of Edward, Harold has himself crowned instead of William.
  - When William hears, he decides to cross the English Channel and claim his throne.
  - Harold is struck in the eye by an arrow and dies in the battle.
- During World War II, it was in the possession of the Germans, who eventually relocated the tapestry to the Louvre in France for safe keeping.
- Today, the tapestry is frequently parodied and used in memes.
  - Tourism Ireland has woven a 250’ Game of Thrones
  - It has been referenced in various movies and cartoons.
- The Goths were a supposed “barbaric” people who held power in various regions of Europe during the Middle Ages. c. 5th-8th centuries.
  - Having been a nomadic people, the Goths were not known for architecture
  - The name Gothic was applied to a style of architecture by individuals and historians after the fact.
    - The Gothic style was a grand departure from previous architectural styles that had preceded it.
    - Gothic style came after the Romanesque style
    - Relative peace, prosperity and stability allowed for new, grand and experimental building programs between roughly 1000 - 1400 CE.
- The Gothic period is particularly notable as essentially a French architectural innovation.
  - It moved from France throughout Europe.
  - Each country added its own particular Gothic aesthetic vision.
  - In France the pursuit was for a vertical cathedral interior which would mirror the “heavenly city of God”, this led to innovations in structural elements.
    - The ribbed vault allowed for the interior space to be lightened both architecturally and aesthetically.
    - These vaults opened interior space and created a visual presentation that was light, airy and filled with stained glass windows.
    - These windows created an interior program that reflected the tone of the period.
    - One of the most notable characteristics of Gothic are the pointed arches.
      - Possibly influenced by Islamic architecture from the Iberian Peninsula.
      - Pointed arches reduced outward thrust, and allowed for slimmer columns/piers
      - Late Romanesque architecture began to experiment with pointed arches and ribbed vaulting.
    - Slimmer columns gave a lighter feel than those of Romanesque churches.
      - The same feel was repeated in the nave gallery and clerestory, and helped to convey a heavenly sense of space that extended from the floor to the vault
      - Reduced thrust and slimmer columns allowed for more and larger windows, bringing in more light than ever before.
      - As the Gothic style grew, so did the ornamentation of the buildings. Complex designs adorn the windows, tracery, and carvings of these buildings.
      - Even the ribbed vaulting grew increasingly complex in their designs.
- There was also an important change in the intellectual perspective of the period
  - women were no longer considered “handmaidens of the devil”
    - The cult of the Virgin replaced a previous veneration of Eve, the great sinner.
    - The image of the Virgin became a central figure and was placed on the great portal entries to the cathedrals where she became a symbol for salvation, redemption and mercy.
  - French celebrated poetry and music dedicated to “love”
  - European boundaries and countries evolved
- In Architecture/Art
  - Portal sculptures develop from stiff, columnar figures with little or no movement to more natural figures
  - Portraiture take shape as individuals commission works for various sections of the cathedrals of Europe
  - Manuscript or book production shifted from a monastic activity to a secular activity.
  - Paris supported important guilds which created manuscripts for profit.
  - Slowly the period transformed secular and religious life in Europe, with a productive relationship established between the Church and secular society
- With the renewed interest in classicism of the 1500’s, the Gothic style yielded to the clean lines and geometry of Neo-classical style.
  - This was when the name “Gothic” was applied, as the style had fallen out of favor with the Renaissance culture.
