# 81 — Frontispiece of the Codex Mendoza

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Indigenous scribes (tlacuiloque). 
**Date:** c. 1541–1542 CE
**Medium:** Ink and color on paper
**Location:** Mexico City, Mexico

## Form

- Work on paper with ink
- Turquoise border with “X”
  - schematic diagram of Tenochtitlan, with the city divided into four parts by intersecting blue-green undulating diagonals. The city was made of canals, similar to the Italian city of Venice, and was divided into four quarters.
- Eagle on cactus
- Figures
- Crops
- Below the schematic diagram of the city are two scenes of military conquest. The artist emphasizes the military power of the Aztecs by showing two soldiers in hierarchical scale

## Function

- History of the Aztecs for Charles V of the Holy Roman Empire
  - He never saw it as it wound up in the hands of French pirates

## Content

- Work divided into three sections
  - History of the Aztecs
    - information about the organization and foundation of the Aztec capital, Tenochtitlan
  - Listing of the towns conquered by the Triple Alliance (three city-states that controlled the area until the defeat by the Spanish conquistadors
  - Activities of daily life

## Context

- The book was named after Don Antonio de Mendoza (1495-1552), the viceroy of New Spain, who supervised its creation and who was a leading patron of native artists.
- Spanish viceroyalty is a political entity under the control of Spain, ruled by a viceroy

- There is a combination of old and new world traditions with respect to art.

  - Native artists still preferred their own traditions of flattened space and earth tones
- A world wide trade network developed and there is an exchange of ideas and materials that influenced styles
- Intermarriage of Native Americans with Spanish results in offspring known as mestizos
