# 30 — Audience Hall (apadana) of Darius and Xerxes

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Persian (Achaemenid). 
**Date:** c. 520–465 BCE
**Medium:** Limestone
**Location:** Persepolis, Iran

## Form

- Hypostyle hall called apandana
- There were 72 Columns with  either eagles, lions or 2 headed bulls atop, scrolls similar to ionic column, 2 more scrolls further down, shaft, base
- Wooden roof supported by cradle created by opposing bull heads
- Glazed brickwork
- Lamassu style beasts at entryways

## Function

- This was a hall for the king to receive tribute from subjects
- The reliefs and sculptures inside and out would serve to intimidate visitors and communicate the power of the king
- This was a symbol of the king’s power

## Content

- Relief carvings of leaders from 23 annexed/conquered countries bringing gifts to the Persian king
- Two headed bulls atop columns signifies power to visitors to the king
- Additional images of power within the complex
- Guards
- Griffins: head and wings of eagle, lions body
- sphinxes: winged monster with woman’s head and a lion's body

## Context

- The hall was begun in the oldest phase of building the palace complex
- Location was high upon a hill so it was easily seen
- Persians were at the height of their power and controlled a large area including parts of Egypt, up to Turkey, across Mesopotamia into modern day Afghanistan, Pakistan and NW India
