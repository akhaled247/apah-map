# 20 — Temple of Amun-Re and Hypostyle Hall

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** New Kingdom, 18th and 19th Dynasties, Egypt. 
**Date:** Temple: c. 1550 BCE; hall: c. 1250 BCE
**Medium:** Cut sandstone and mud brick
**Location:** Karnak, near Luxor, Egypt

## Form

- The main temple of Amun-Re had two axes—one that went north/south and the other that extended east/west.
- The temple is an example of a hypostyle hall - this is a space with a roof supported by columns
  - There are 134 columns supporting the roof
  - The 12 center columns are 69’ tall (roughly 6 ⅓  stories high)
  - The column would have been brightly painted
- The center of the hall is taller than the two sides allowing for clerestory illumination of the hall

## Function

- Karnak was a place of worship, but also a place for the god Amun-Re to dwell on earth.
- Residence for priests

## Content

- Conceptually, Egyptian temples were related to the creation of the world
  - They were visual representations of the “mound of creation” emerging from primeval waters
  - Ceilings represented the sky and were often painted with birds
  - Columns were designed with lotus, papyrus, and palm plants in order to reflect the marsh-like environment of creation

## Context

- Construction first begun in the Middle Kingdom
- Karnak is a temple precinct located near Luxor (ancient city Thebes)
  - Thebes was capital of Egypt during the New Kingdom
- Successive pharaohs added to the complex for over 2000 years
- Even today, Karnak is one of the largest temple complexes in the world
- The tallest obelisk in Egypt stood at Karnak and was dedicated by the female pharaoh Hatshepsut who ruled Egypt during the New Kingdom.It originally had a matching obelisk that was removed by the Roman emperor Constantine and re-erected in Rome.
- The earliest examples of clerestory lighting comes from Egypt
