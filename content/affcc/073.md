# 73 — Last Supper

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Leonardo da Vinci. 
**Date:** c. 1494–1498 CE
**Medium:** Oil and tempera on plaster
**Location:** Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan, Italy

## Form

- Wall Painting, new technique
  - Mix of oil and tempera giving opportunity to paint on dry plaster
  - Gives longer “open” time
  - Was able to work all over instead of small sections at a time
- The placement of Christ over the vanishing point and the subsequent convergence of the orthogonal lines, direct the viewer straight to Christ.
- Disciples are positioned in 4 groupings of 3 as opposed to being lined up in a row.

## Content

- Christ and disciples in the upper room having Christ’s final meal
- Christ wearing the traditional red and blue, yet missing the halo.
  - Some interpret the window behind to serve as the halo, others suggest that the halo is missing as Christ is still human at this point
- Characters can be identified by symbolic traits

## Context

- Also known as Cenacolo (Chin-oc-o-la), in latin this is the “place where you eat,” but in Italian, it has become synonymous with images of Christ’s last supper with his disciples.
- This is one of the best known paintings in the world
- His new technique, along with environmental factors have led to the deterioration of the painting and consequently has required numerous restorations
  - Little is left of the original painting.
  - The work had suffered considerable damage in the short years following its completion
  - Blurring and flaking of paint
  - Since the early 18th century, we have records of nine attempts at restoration
  - The last restoration attempt took place from 1977 - 1999
- Around 1495, Ludovico Sforza, then the Duke of Milan, commissioned da Vinci to paint “The Last Supper”
- The painting’s location is in the dining room of the former Dominican convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan
- during the French Revolutionary War Napoleon's troops used the wall of the refectory to make target practice
- during the Second World War in 1943 the bombings managed to tear off the roof of the old Dominican dining room leaving the paint in the open for several years.
- This was a popular subject for dining areas of convents and monasteries.
- About the Artist, Leonardo Da Vinci
  - Painter, architect, sculptor, engineer, inventor, mathematician, anatomist and writer
  - While painting this mural, he was also busy with studies of light, sound, movements and emotions, may be why these same ideas find their way into the painting
  - Little formal education
  - Around 14, became an apprentice to Andrea del Verrocchio in Florence
  - Abandoned some of his commissions without completing them.
  - Kept hundreds of notebooks, full of anatomical studies, engineering ideas, wrote backwards
  - fewer than two dozen paintings attributed to Da Vinci exist
