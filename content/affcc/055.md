# 55 — Lindisfarne Gospels (St. Matthew, cross-carpet page; St. Luke, incipit page)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Eadfrith, Bishop of Lindisfarne. 
**Date:** c. 700 CE
**Medium:** Illuminated manuscript (ink, pigments, and gold on vellum)
**Location:** Lindisfarne (Holy Island), Northumbria, England

## Form

- Illuminated manuscripts, handmade object
- Contains five highly elaborate full-page carpet pages
  - Design is akin to metalwork found within the time period
  - Insular or Hiberno-saxon style;  contains influences from the Anglo-Saxons, the Celts, the Romans, the Byzantines, and the Copts, demonstrating the multicultural society that was Britain in the seventh century
  - Cross/abstract animals
  - Get their name from their similarity in appearance to carpets eastern Mediterranean
- 4 Carpet pages alongside the “Incipit” pages
  - Incipit pages mark the beginning of the gospel with decorated text
- 1 Carpet page along prefatory pages
  - Prefatory materials are introductory materials in a speech or book.
- full-page images of the four Evangelists

- Colophon, or inscription that provides valuable evidence of the manuscript’s production.

## Context

- During the period following the fall of the western Roman Empire, warring groups struggled for political control over much of Europe. With increased travel, and the spread of Christianity, a mix of ideas, creativity and styles spread
- The Lindisfarne Gospels were  created in a monastery from North Umbria, England.
  - Considered the most spectacular manuscript to survive from Anglo Saxon England
- Prior to the invention of the printing press in the 15th century, all books were created by hand.
- Creation of full bibles by single monasteries were rare, and most would gather books into separate volumes.
- In the late-10th century, additions were made to the manuscript by a priest named Aldred (active c. 970)
