# 16 — Standard of Ur from the Royal Tombs at Ur

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Sumerian. 
**Date:** c. 2600–2400 BCE
**Medium:** Wood inlaid with shell, lapis lazuli, and red limestone
**Location:** Tell el-Muqayyar (modern Iraq)

## Form

- When viewed from the front or back, it is rectangular, but when seen from the end, it appears as a truncated triangle (shortened or missing the tip in this case)
- Size is approximately 8 ⅝” tall and approximately 19 ⅞” in length
- Lapis lazuli from Afghanistan, Shells from Persian Gulf, Red limestone from India
- Three registers on each side
- Hierarchical scale used for ruler
- Figures represented have broad shoulders with heads and feet in profile
- Large eyes similar to the votive figures from Eshnunna
- There had been decay from burial, so it has been restored

## Function

- Real function is unknown, but one theory suggests that it was carried on a pole and hence the name.
- Another suggests it was the soundbox of a musical instrument

## Content

- War Side
  - Bottom register - chariots are trampling the enemy
  - Middle register - soldiers on left side, middle they are killing enemy and right naked enemy are being marched to king
  - Top register - King is center with enemy being presented on right
- Peace side
  - Lower two registers: Preparations being made for celebration
  - Upper register: King is in the upper left register. Note hierarchical scale. Guests and musicians on right

## Context

- Materials used in the construction of the Standard of Ur were luxurious and hard to obtain, showing the patron’s wealth/status
- Object was found in one of the largest graves from the Royal cemetery at Ur. Positioned at the right shoulder of the man buried there
- Ur is famous as the home of the Old Testament patriarch Abraham (Genesis 11:29–32), there is no actual proof that Tell el-Muqayyar was identical with “Ur of the Chaldees.”
- “Nanna” was the moon goddess and the patron deity for the city-state of Ur
