# 77 — Isenheim altarpiece

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Matthias Grünewald. 
**Date:** c. 1512–1516 CE
**Medium:** Oil on wood
**Location:** Isenheim (now Colmar), France

## Form

- Polyptych
- Oil on multiple panels, sculpture; essentially a box of statues covered by folding wings
- Images on the outside
- Opens to two different interior views
- Large scale

## Function

- Opened on special occasions
- Focal of the church where the sacrament of the Eucarist or Mass is performed
- Remind people of the physical healing and spiritual salvation of Christ

## Content

- Closed
  - Crucifixion of Christ
    - Illustrates great anguish
    - Diseased skin
    - Emphasis on human qualities
  - St. Anthony on right side
    - Anthony is appealed to against infectious diseases, particularly skin diseases.
  - St. Sebastian on left
    - Martyr
    - In medieval times, he was regarded as a saint with a special ability to intercede to protect from plague
    - was miraculously saved after being shot through with arrows - symbol of Christ’s ability to heal
  - Lamb holding cross - reference to the “Lamb of God” (Christ and his death on the cross)
  - John the Baptist - (on our right) symbol of Christ sacrifice
  - On our left is Mary (mother) and John (disciple who Christ entrusted with the care of his mother)
- Predella - lamentation image
- Open
  - Position 1 - 4 panels depict the Virgin and Christ
    - Left - annunciation
    - Center left - celebration of angels
    - Center right - the nativity
    - Right - Resurrected Christ
  - Position 2 -
    - Predella - last supper
    - Center sculpture
      - St. Anthony - Bearded and enthroned
      - two of the four great fathers of the Latin Church
        - St. Augustine on our left
        - St Jerome on our right
      - Guy Guers - patron kneeling

## Context

- Made for the Monastery of St. Anthony in Isenheim, which had a hospital that treated, among other ailments, the skin disease known as St. Anthony's fire.
- Sculpted wooden altars were popular in Germany at the time
- Immediately after the High Renaissance period, from 1520 until 1600
- Mannerism discards the ideas of linear perspective held in the Renaissance, by eliminating the “grid” included in many paintings and creating a confused space with tightly grouped figures, the eye is encouraged to move freely about the picture plane.
- Human forms were elongated and able to move and perform in ways physically impossible.
- Vivid or garish color is often used
- During this period, still life along with other genre paintings gained acceptance as a legitimate art form.
