# 32 — Tomb of the Triclinium

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Etruscan. 
**Date:** c. 480–470 BCE
**Medium:** Tufa and fresco
**Location:** Tarquinia, Italy

## Form

- A chamber tomb (subterranean burial chamber)
- In the tufa regions of Etruria, tombs were cut from stone
- Wall decor (from the bottom up):
- Wave-like pattern along the bottom of the walls
- horizontal bands with the top serving as the ground for the scenes
- Figures/animals
- Style is comparable to Greek vases of the 6th century
- Banquet scene with guests, dancers and musicians
- Females are rendered lighter in tone than the men
- One wall contains young men jumping down from horses
- Banqueteers recline on three couches
- Small trees separate the figures
- There is a cat prowling after a rooster and a partridge
- Above the figures, there is another band filled with a wavy line running down the middle. Smaller patterns fill the band.
- A checkerboard pattern fills the vaulted portions of the ceiling with a flat portion running down the middle and separating the two.
- The center of the ceiling has 4 bands of color (two on each side) with a circular pattern in the middle section.

## Function

- Funerary, an example of a painted chamber tomb
- Purpose was to house not only the deceased, but various grave goods and offerings
- The celebration helped to reinforce the socio-economic position of the deceased person and his/her family.

## Content

- The paintings reveal information about burial traditions, but also cultural activities.

## Context

- Etruscans amassed great wealth as a result of natural materials used during the iron age, engaging in medium, to far reaching trade.
- The name Triclenium comes from the Roman name of a formal dining room. Tri \= 3 and clinium \= couch for reclining
- Funerary contexts constitute the most abundant archaeological evidence for the Etruscan civilization.
- Their temples were made of non-permanent materials and have mostly deteriorated
- There is, for unknown reasons, little in the way of written history for the Etruscan civilization.
- Elaborate funerary rituals were engaged in the by the elites of society
- Etruscan funeral rites were not somber but festive, with the aim of sharing a final meal with the deceased as the latter transitioned to the afterlife.
