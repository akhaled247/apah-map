# 27 — Anavysos Kouros

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Archaic Greek. 
**Date:** c. 530 BCE
**Medium:** Marble with remnants of paint
**Location:** Anavysos, Greece

## Form

- Slightly larger than life (6’4.5”)
- More natural modeling than older works from the archaic period
- Has the “archaic smile”
- Hands firmly at side, with left leg slightly forward
- Nude

## Function

- Often used for grave markers
- Sometimes used as a dedication to the gods in sanctuaries

## Content

- These sculptures were common, typically nude, and shared common features - stance for example.
- Not necessarily a portrait, but a depiction of the ideal youthful male
- a combination of moral and physical beauty and nobility

## Context

- This example was found in Anavysos, Attica (a village in southwestern Attica). Attica is a district in ancient Greece that was also the location of Athens
- There are characteristics that appear to be influences from Egyptian sculpture: body is stiff and straight, left leg forward, arms are close to sides
- While Egyptian statues often were carved against a flat slab and at least partially clothed, Greek Kouros were freestanding and always nude, a feature that helped detach the Kouros from a specific historical setting.
