# 46 — Pantheon

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Imperial Roman. 
**Date:** 118–125 CE
**Medium:** Concrete with stone facing
**Location:** Rome, Italy

## Form

- The original version was thought to be a rectangular temple, Greek in its plan.
- The latest version has the front of a traditional temple with pediment, 16 columns and the pediment names Marcus Agrippa as the patron.
- The front of the building is a portico, with monolithic Corinthian columns, and a pediment
- The large bronze doors to the cella, measuring 14.6 ft wide by 24.7 ft high
- It is a rotunda; in other words, a round building
- There were niches in the walls that would have held sculptures of gods and deified emperors.
- They were framed with a column on the left and right
- Those columns supported an entablature above which there is a curved pediment.
- It has a domed interior with a coffered ceiling
- The Pantheon’s dome is the largest in the ancient world, 142 feet across.
- Built with concrete using various size and weights of rock to control strength and weight of the dome
- The oculus (27’ in diameter) helps with weight concerns but also provides  light for the interior.
- Walls are some 20’ thick to help bear the weight and outward thrust of the dome.
- The dome is a hemisphere; the building was designed such that a perfect sphere would fit from the floor to the top of the dome.
- The frieze above the columns has a series of false windows with pediments alternating with rectangular shapes. These do not align with the coffered ceiling, which creates a dynamic feel, almost as if the dome is rotating.
- Rectangles, squares and geometric shapes are used throughout the interior
- Colored marble

## Function

- Temple
- Church
- Various individuals have been buried there.
- To instill a sense of pride in Roman culture

## Content

- Name: a temple for all Roman deities
- prefix “pan” meaning "all", "of everything", or "involving all members" of a group.
- Theon means godly and is of Greek origin
- Design is about earth and heavenly spheres meeting
- Gods surrounding
- Dome representing heavens
- Oculus the sun
- However - the above information is now under question. New findings indicate that it may not have been a temple, but had other uses.
- Expense of materials (colored marble) shows wealth and power of the Roman Empire to reach across the world and import materials
- Movement of light from the oculus, through the space of the rotunda

## Context

- Originally built by Marcus Agrippa in 27 BC, son-in-law of Emperor Augustus) in 27 BCE.; burning in 80 CE.
- Rebuilt or restored in the same location by Emperor Domitian (emperor from 81-96 CE) ; but struck by lightning and burned again in 110 CE
- It was thought that Emperor Hadrian was ruler when construction began on the final version, but new research has shown that it might have been his predecessor, Trajan.
- The Pantheon has been in continuous use since its final construction in c AD 126-128
- The Pantheon fell into disrepair, as did many Roman buildings, after Constantine moved the center of the empire from Rome to Constantinople.
- With the rise of Chirstianity, the building was abandoned and pillaged by the Goths
- In 609 CE, it was dedicated as a Christian church, possibly saving it from ruin as the church spent a great deal repairing and maintaining the structure.
- It was the first Roman temple to be consecrated as a church.
- Pantheon continues to function as a church, as well as a major tourist destination
- This is one of the most copied buildings in the Renaissance and in modern times.
- Thomas Jefferson modeled both Monticello—his home near Charlottesville, Virginia—as well as the Rotunda building at the University of Virginia, after the Pantheon.
