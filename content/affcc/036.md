# 36 — Grave stele of Hegeso

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Attributed to Kallimachos. 
**Date:** c. 410 BCE
**Medium:** Marble and paint
**Location:** Dipylon Cemetery, Kerameikos, Athens, Greece

## Form

- Large stone 5’2”
- General shape is rectangular with a triangular top.
- Represents a house
- Two columns (or pillistars) on each side of the stele with a pediment and inscription
- Acroterion (ak-row-tear-e-un) are at the apex of the pediment and the outside corners
- Two women, Hegeso is seated and one (the servant) standing; both looking downward toward something in the seated women’s hand (a necklace that was originally painted in, but now is obviously gone)
- They are holding a jewelry box
- Curved back and legs of the chair, footstool under Hegeso’s left foot. These items are in profile view.
- Faces in profile with torsos turned slightly toward the viewer.
- Feet are also foreshortened.
- Style is similar to that of the Phidias style
- Clothing that drapes and closely follows the form.
- Attention to detail
- Depth is created by overlapping forms (legs and feet) as well as the differences in the depth of the relief.
- Note the implied lines within the composition.
- From where the pilasters meet the bottom of the pediment, through the gaze of the figures, meeting at the hand holding the necklace, down the legs on the left side and the leg of the chair on the right.
- The vertical line going from the center acroterion, though the hand and down to the leg of the chair.
- The horizontal line that is created by the connection of the hands holding the box.
- All this creates a very safe and stable composition that creates a feeling of peace. .

## Function

- Funerary - grave stone

## Content

- Hegeso (seated) is presented with a jewelry box by her  servent (standing) and is examining
- The setting is within the home

## Context

- Toward the end of the high classical period of Greece there is a renewed interest in funerary sculpture
- In the archaic period wealthy patrons commissioned kouroi (core-roy) and korai (core-rye) as grave markers.
- This type of work mostly disappeared during the high classical period when the state was the primary patron of sculpture.

- Women were not considered citizens, and their status was tied to the husband or dominant male (father)
- The inscription below the pediment says, “Hegeso, daughter of Proxenos.”
