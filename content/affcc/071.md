# 71 — Madonna and Child with Two Angels

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Fra Filippo Lippi. 
**Date:** c. 1465 CE
**Medium:** Tempera on wood
**Location:** Florence, Italy

## Form

- Tempera - on gessoed board
  - Doesn’t allow for mixing on ground like oil
  - Requires precise brushwork
  - Dries quickly to a matt finish
  - Gloss obtained through a varnish
- Composition is similar to numerous relief sculptures made by the Florentine sculptors of the same period.
- Use of atmospheric perspective in the landscape
- Use of the color blue was common in depictions of Mary, representing her royalty from the Byzantine era onward.
- Angels look like children
- Halo is less prominent
- A very earthly appearance to divine subjects

## Function

- This was an altarpiece

## Content

- Madonna and child with two angels
  - Mary’s hands clasped in prayer
  - Melancholy look - foreshadowing?
- Playful when compared to similar subjects from the 14th century
- Humanist approach
- Clothes and hairstyles inspired by the fashions of the day

## Context

- Artist: Florentine painter in the second generation of Renaissance artists
- born into a very large and poor family
- Raised by aunt before being placed in monastery that was being decorated with frescoes by Masaccio
  - These paintings were among the most glorious and influential paintings of the Renaissance
- Kidnapped by the Moors
  - Moors were the Muslim inhabitants of the Maghreb, the Iberian Peninsula, Sicily, and Malta during the Middle Ages
  - held as a slave for 18 months, and then freed after he painted a portrait of his owner
- Spent time in Padua after which he returned to Florence where he was protected by the Medici family
- Lippi was a teacher of Bottocelli
- Humanism during the renaissance
  - Renaissance humanism was a revival in the study of classical antiquity, at first in Italy and then spreading across Western Europe in the 14th, 15th, and 16th centuries.
  - During the Renaissance period most humanists were religious, so their concern was to "purify and renew Christianity," not to do away with it. Their vision was to return to the sources, to the simplicity of the New Testament, bypassing the complexities of medieval theology.
  - Today, by contrast, the term humanism has come to signify "a worldview which denies the existence or relevance of God, or which is committed to a purely secular outlook.
