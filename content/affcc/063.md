# 63 — Arena (Scrovegni) Chapel, including Lamentation

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Giotto di Bondone. 
**Date:** Chapel: c. 1303 CE; Fresco: c. 1305 CE
**Medium:** Brick (architecture) and fresco
**Location:** Padua, Italy

## Form

- Theatrical settings and poses; creation of architectural type sets with the illusion of depth and space
- Images are sequential, using registers
- Brightly colored as well as grisaille images
- Movement back to modeling and the illusion of depth
- Independent images are set into registers that read like a narrative.
- Between the image panels are trompe l'oeil, faux marble panels
- Materials
  - Brick, wood(?)
  - Clean and simple exterior, simple arches
  - 3 Lancet combined with 2 six-lobed rose windows
  - 6 windows along the southern wall
  - Rosette window in the apse
- Design Elements/principles utilized and where?
  - Contrasting color/value between the brick and the white trim
  - Small repeating arches across the gabel and down the side walls creates a visual rhythm
  - Alternating color of brick in the arch over the doors
  - Unity/Variety with the use of white accents/trimwork
  - the chapel presents very simple architecture: a rectangular hall with a barrel vault, an elegant gothic triple lancet window on the façade, tall, narrow windows on the southern wall, and a polygonal apse, later raised to contain the belfry"

## Function

- This is a religious narrative depicting the life of Christ and his mother Mary, starting with Joachim and Anna
- Served as a private chapel, though this changed later.
- Chapel
- Scrovegni built as a intersession for his “sin” of usury
- Was originally part of the the Scrovegni Palace

## Content

- The frescoes follow three main themes:
  - episodes in the lives of Joachim and Anna (1-6)
    - From the Apocrypha
    - Tradition says they are the parents of Mary, therefore the grandparents of Christ
  - episodes in the Virgin Mary's life (7-13),
  - episodes recounting Christ's life and death.
- The lower parts of the walls contain a series of frescoes illustrating Vices and Virtues in allegory. (These are the grisaille images.)
- The wall opposite the altar is the grandiose Universal Judgment, which concludes the story of human salvation.
- Exterior is simple compared to Gothic standard
- Free from content

## Context

- Considered by some to be the beginning of the Renaissance, a Proto-Renaissance. The images here were a distinctive change from Gothic style images.
- This marked the beginning of a revolution in mural painting and influenced fresco technique, style, and content for a whole century.
- dedicated to St. Mary of the Charity, frescoed between 1303 and 1305 by Giotto, upon the commission of Enrico degli Scrovegni
  - Scrovegni was a wealthy money lender
  - It was meant to be a small private chapel and was connected to a large palace
  - It is possible that he was giving the church/chapel as a way to “buy” his way into heaven.
- Chapel was acquired by the City of Padova in1880, and the vulnerable frescoes were subjected to several specialized restoration operations during the 19th and 20th centuries.
- Chapel was part of a larger complex
- Built by a banker named Scrovegni
- Chapel backs up to a Roman amphitheater
- Adjacent palace was destroyed for materials and condos built in its place.
- Time: late Gothic/Proto-Renaissance
