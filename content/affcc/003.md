# 3 — Camelid sacrum in the shape of a canine

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Upper Paleolithic hunter-gatherers. 
**Date:** 14,000–7000 BCE
**Medium:** Carved bone (camelid sacrum)
**Location:** Tequixquiac, central Mexico

## Form

- Hand carved bone from camelid (animal related to camels and llamas)
- The bone was a natural choice as it already bears a resemblance to the subject
- It is nearly symmetrical
- Natural coloration

## Function

- Possibly had some sacred or religious purpose as the sacrum held importance to later meso American cultures.  This may have been the reason that this particular bone was chosen. It held spiritual significance.
- Created before the development of written language, it is impossible to know for certain the meaning to the artist.

## Content

- The head of a dog carved from the sacrum of a camelid. The sacrum is a bone at the base of the spine

## Context

- Currently housed in Mexico City's National Museum of Anthropology
- Most likely carved by a nomadic hunter of large game during the upper paleolithic period, it is one of the oldest examples of art from the Americas
- Discovered in 1870 in Tequixquiac, just north of Mexico City by an engineer working on a drainage project
- Difficult to date as stratigraphic analysis wasn’t done at the site during the time of discovery.
