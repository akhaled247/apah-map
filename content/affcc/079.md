# 79 — Allegory of Law and Grace

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Lucas Cranach the Elder. 
**Date:** c. 1530 CE
**Medium:** Woodcut and letterpress
**Location:** Wittenberg, Germany

## Form

- Woodcut print, (work on paper)
- Achromatic, black (and white of paper)
- Picture plane divided into two halves horizontally
  - The halves show balance, but not pure symmetry.
- Line is used extensively, both for contour and for modeling of the form.
  - Both implied and actual line moves the viewer's eye through the composition
    - Circular implied line in clouds that surround God on the left panel
    - Actual line that moves the eye from the clouds to Christ on cross, to the man in the lower section of the right panel.

## Function

- This served to communicate the Luther’s idea of grace vs the Catholic churches’ idea of law
- Prints allowed for rapid dissemination of ideas.

## Content

- The law (left) side shows damnation through the law
- The grace (right) side shows salvation through grace.

## Context

- Lucas Cranach was a friend and follower of Martin Luther
- The reformation started as a reform movement but quickly became a rebellion as people questioned the authority and ways of the Catholic Church.
