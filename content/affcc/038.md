# 38 — Great Altar of Zeus and Athena at Pergamon

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Hellenistic Greek. 
**Date:** c. 175 BCE
**Medium:** Marble (architecture and sculpture)
**Location:** Pergamon (modern Bergama, Turkey)

## Form

- The temple was built upon an elevated platform
- On either side of the altar were Ionic, stoa-like projections
- Along the altar’s platform was a massive frieze, sculpted in high relief and nearly 400’ in length and 7.5’ in height
- The frieze contains around 100 figures whose scale is larger than life.
- The narrative frieze wrapped around all sides of the temple and even makes it way up the stairs
- The stairs become part of the scene as the  figures move out onto the steps.
- The high relief creates dark shadows and a real sense of depth
- The sculpture is filled with a variety of angles and overlapping of forms, creating a tremendous action throughout.
- This is definitely Hellenistic art.
- difference between Hellenistic and Classical Art : The Hellenistic period saw emotions, movement of figures whereas in the Classical period there is more focus on the perfect realistic figures, the sculptures are static
- There is a colonnade (row of columns supporting a roof) that goes around the entire perimeter

## Function

- Commemorative; celebrating the victory of King Attalos I over an invasion by the Gauls.
- Religion, mythological

## Content

- The frieze conveys the story of Zeus and the gods in battle with the giants for control of the earth known as gigantomachy. (ge-gan-tom-ah-key)
- The battle represented a crucial shift for the ancient Greeks: the old religion, which was rooted in the natural world (for example, Ge, the earth goddess and mother of the giants), was overthrown by the new, civilized order of the Olympian gods (for example, Zeus, Athena, and others). According to the myth, the gods received a prophecy that the giants could only be defeated with the help of a mortal. Zeus (assisted by Athena), called upon the hero Herakles who dealt the decisive blow by shooting them with arrows.
- This likely represents the Attalid military victories over the Gauls, a Celtic tribe from Western Europe.
- The sections of the frieze that contain Athena and the one that has Zeus, show both as being in control though fighting several of the giants at once.

## Context

- After the death of Alexander the Great and the breakup of his empire, the kingdom of Attalos II was born. The Pergamene empire controlled most of western and southern Asia Minor.
- The Altar once stood in a sacred precinct on the acropolis of the ancient city of Pergamon (on the west coast of modern-day Turkey).
- Viewers would have approached from the back side of the temple, following the scene along around the front and even up the stairs.
- The Altar of Zeus and Athena at Pergamon is most famous Hellenistic architectural example
- After its discovery in the 14th century CE, and again in the 19th century, a German team excavated the site.
- It was purchased by the Germans from the Ottomans.
- The temple was dismantled, and moved to Germany where the facade was reconstructed.
- During WWII, it was moved to a bunker for protection.
- After the war, it was claimed by the Soviet Union and moved to St. Petersburg.
- Eventually it was returned to Berlin in 1958\.
