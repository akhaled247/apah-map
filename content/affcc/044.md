# 44 — Colosseum (Flavian Amphitheater)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Imperial Roman. 
**Date:** 70–80 CE
**Medium:** Stone and concrete
**Location:** Rome, Italy

## Form

- Oval shaped structure.
- There are design elements pulled from ancient Greek temples.
- Different orders of columns are stacked
- Bottom - Similar to the Doric, but is actually a Tuscan order
- Middle - Ionic order
- Third and fourth are Corinthian
- Top uses pilasters, instead of columns.
- Arches surround the building on the bottom three stories.
- There are 80 arched entrances on the bottom level.
- of those are numbered corresponding to your ticket
- The 4 entrances facing the cardinal directions had inscriptions from Vespasian saying this was a gift to the people of Rome built with the spoils from the Jewish war.
- The emperor had his own entrance.
- A complex series of arches, hallways and stairs allowed for spectators to move to their seats.
- Barrel vaults were present on the first floor of the colosseum, lining the entire outer perimeter.
- Groined vaults were constructed in the interior of the structure
- There was a level under the stadium floor with elevators and trap doors were animals and props were raised to ground level for the events
- An awning (called a velarium) surrounded the top of the colosseum. It was operated by ropes and provided some shade for viewers

## Function

- A Stadium
- Exotic animal hunts
- Execution of prisoners, including some Christians
- Gladiator fights
- As a result of the martyrdom of Christians, it became a pilgrimage site by later visitors.

## Context

- This is one the most renowned monuments from ancient Rome
- Seeing the colosseum in ruins is a present day reminder of the death of ancient Rome
- The structure is set upon the location of an artificial lake that Nero had in the center of  his palace grounds.
- The colosseum was built by a later emperor, Vespasian who immediately followed Nero.
- Nero was highly unpopular and eventually committed suicide in 68 CE.  (AD68)
- The Flavians were Italian gentry
- Gentry are people of good social position, specifically (in the UK) and the class of people next below the nobility in position and birth.
- They brought stability back to Rome after a time of social unrest and civil wars.
- Vespasian gave the land that the palace was seated on back to the city of Rome.
- The original name was not The Colosseum, but rather the Flavian Amphitheater.
- During the middle ages it became known as The Colosseum due to the giant, or colossal statue of the sun god next to the stadium. It was approximately 100’ tall. (roughly the height of a 10 story building, or a little under the length of 3 school buses parked end to end)
- The term amphitheater means double theater. This is because the design resembled two Greek theaters put together, creating an oval shape.
- The architecture of the colosseum is known for its use of arches, a Roman innovation.
- It is estimated to hold about 50,000 spectators.
- VIPs, the emperor, judges, priests, etc sat in the lowest section.
- Senators sat behind them, wealthy business men behind them.
- Even further up were the common people and the cheap seats in the nosebleed section were for slaves, women and foriegners.
- The amphitheater has fallen victim to numerous earthquakes and stone pillaging from later Romans who used the stone in their own homes and other buildings.
- The design of the Colosseum influences sporting and entertainment venues today
