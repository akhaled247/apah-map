# 61 — Dedication Page with Blanche of Castile and King Louis IX of France, Scenes from the Apocalypse from Bibles moralisées

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Gothic Europe. 
**Date:** c. 1225–1245 CE
**Medium:** Illuminated manuscript (ink, tempera, and gold leaf on vellum)
**Location:** Paris, France

## Form

- Materials:
  - Made from vellum (calfskin)
  - Ink
  - Tempera
  - Gold leaf
- Illustrations
- Text
  - Biblical
  - Commentary
    - Called gloss
    - Interprets text for the reader
- Structure of pages organized the text/commentary and illustrations
- Dedication page - broken into quadrants
  - Columns
  - Arches - (Trefoil)
  - Skewed perspective in furniture and architecture
  - Gold leaf ground with blues/greens/orange colors
  - Top two registers - patron with king
  - Monk and scribe in bottom
- Manuscript
  - Each page has eight circles, called roundels, that illustrate biblical scenes and commentary scenes.
  - Illustrations accompany each body of text

## Function

- Spiritual communication and education to largely illiterate populations

## Content

- Biblical illustrations
- Biblical text
- Commentary
  - Anti semitism was a common theme in the commentary
  - Explanations of scripture
  - Parallels between the 13th century times and scripture

## Context

- Made in France and Spain
- These bibles were limited in number
- Patrons were wealthy
  - With so many illustrations, the cost was significant
  - Usually made for royal families
- Anti semitism prevalent in Medieval Europe
- Different versions of the Bibles Moralisees
- This version divided into 3 pieces located in three different cities
