# 75 — Sistine Chapel ceiling and altar wall frescoes

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Michelangelo. 
**Date:** Ceiling: 1508–1512 CE; altar wall: 1536–1541 CE
**Medium:** Fresco
**Location:** Vatican City (Rome, Italy)

## Form

- General:
  - Buon fresco, with possibility of fresco secco for some detail work.
  - Brightly colored
  - Size: 133′ 0″ x 46′ 0″
  - Scenes set within actual and faux architectural divisions
  - Figures are masculine, with great volume and muscularity
- Delphic Sibyl
  - Wears a Greek style turban
  - Head turned, giving attention to something outside our view
  - Holds a scroll containing prophecy
  - Seated within architectural recess/bench with nude behind and to her left
- The Flood
  - Picture plane is divided into four different sections
    - Ark in the background
    - Capsizing boat in middle
    - People huddled in makeshift shelter together in lower right
    - Lower left people are headed for higher ground

## Function

- The chapel where new popes are elected and papel services take place
- Prayer chapel for the pope

## Content

- Frescos by a variety of renaissance artists adorn the walls with the main focus the ceiling paintings by Michelangelo
- Religious imagery from old testament on ceiling
  - Center panels include
    - Creation
    - Creation of man/the fall
    - Stories of Noah
  - Others
    - Family of Jesus
    - Prophets
    - Sybils
    - Various old testament stories

## Context

- The chapel was built in 1472 and painted by Quattrocento artists (1400-1499 or roughly the 15th century equivalent)
- Paintings from the quattrocento were Christ and Moses, Michelangelo opted for a different theme - creation/the fall/Noah
- Paintings were above window level as well as the ceiling, approximately 60’ up (roughly 6 stories)
- The Delphic 	sibyl is one of five on the ceiling
  - These are Greco-Roman figures who believed foretold the coming of Christ
  - Illustrates a mix of Christian and pagan mythological imagery
