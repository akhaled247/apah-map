# 60 — Chartres Cathedral

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Gothic Europe. 
**Date:** Original construction c. 1145–1155 CE; reconstructed c. 1194–1220 CE
**Medium:** Limestone, stained glass
**Location:** Chartres, France

## Form

- Gothic Style
  - West facade is Early Gothic
  - South facade and interior is High Gothic
- Cruciform plan
- Wings aligned to the N, S, E, W
- West Facing facade (oldest part of church)
  - Also known as the Royal Portal
  - Three doorways with tympanums
    - Central is slightly larger than two outside portals
    - Each has different scene,
      - Christ in majesty: center
      - Incarnation of Christ (on right)
      - Ascension of Christ (on left)
        - When tower was rebuilt, two of the figures were removed, thus there are 10, not 12 apostles
  - Arches are Early Gothic style that is similar to Romanesque
  - 3 doors - to represent Father, Son, Holy Spirit
  - Tympanums have reliefs representing
    - Christ before human form (on left)
    - Virgin Mary (on right)
    - Second coming of Christ/Judgment of mankind
  - Jamb figures
    - Unknown figures
    - Slender, stiff and frontal
    - Help to emphasize the verticality of the columns
  - The frieze has a horizontal rhythmic pattern created by a row of figures.
  - Two large asymmetrical towers
    - South West was an one original
    - North West one reportedly burned in 1134 and rebuilt in the 16th century, later Gothic style
  - Rose window
  - Three large stained-glass windows
    - Tall is characteristic of Gothic style
    - Rounded top is Romanesque
  - These are small windows when compared with (later) High Gothic style
- North and South Facades
  - Deeper and more ornate
  - Figures are becoming more naturalistic
- Interior
  - Bays
    - Use of piers to support vaults
      - Piers have colonettes
      - Colonettes become ribs
      - Ribs support the groin vaults
      - 2 lancet windows below an oculus
  - Walls
    - Divided into three parts
      - Nave Arcade (bottom section)
      - Triforium (mid section)
      - Clerestory (upper section)
- Stained glass
  - Technique dates as far back as Byzantine, but really developed in 12th and 13th centuries
  - Small pieces of colored glass inserted into lead borders that held them together. Made into panels and inserted into window frames
  - Narrative and didactic
    - Presented gospels, biblical and religious characters and their stories
  - The Blue Virgin Window
- Representative
- Symbolic

## Context

- Constitutes the complete and perfected expression of one of the most characteristic aspects of medieval art.
- exercised considerable influence on the development of Gothic art in France and beyond
- The most elucidating example one could choose to define the cultural, social and aesthetic reality of the Gothic cathedral.
- Destination of a pilgrimage dedicated to the Virgin Mary
  - Relic - tunic worn when Mary birthed Jesus
  - Relic saved when church burned, sign to build larger, grander church
- The west façade built around the middle of the 12th century
- the nave and the choir, reconstructed as of 1194
- Center for learning had influence on and been located next to church
- The north transept window makes reference to both Blanche of Castile and King Louis IX, suggesting that they may have been patrons.
- Blanche of Castile
  - Born in Spain in 1188
  - Wife of Louis the VIII of France
  - Mother of Louis the IX of France
  - Twice regent of France, who by wars and marital alliances did much to secure and unify French territories.
  - Daughter of Alfonso VIII (king of Castile, Spain) and Eleanor, daughter of Henry II of England
  - Grandmother Eleanor of Aquitaine (queen of England) went to Spain, took Blanche to France, for a marriage treaty with Louis (son of King Phillip II Augustus
    - This was arranged by Blanche’s uncle, King John of England
- Louis the IX
  - Also known as Saint Louis
  - Was a popular king, ruled from 1226 - 1270
  - Led the Seventh Crusade to the Holy Lands in 1248-50
  - Died in a later crusade to Tunisia
- Obviously both possessed great status and wealth, and were patrons of the arts, including churches and a moralized bible.
