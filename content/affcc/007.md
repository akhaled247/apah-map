# 7 — Jade cong

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Liangzhu culture. 
**Date:** 3300–2200 BCE
**Medium:** Carved jade
**Location:** Liangzhu, China

## Form

- Made from jade
- Metamorphic rock
- Harder than steel
- Shades of green, white, yellow, red
- Its strength allows it to be carved into intricate designs
- Must be abraded with sand because of hardness
- Shape bears a resemblance to a hollow tube embedded into a rectangular block
- Faces carved into corners
- Lines - repeated with a degree of regularity

## Function

- Some type of ritual object
- Found in graves around the perimeter of a the deceased
- Exact function unknown, possibly to serve a connection between earthly and spiritual realms.

## Content

- Mask-like faces, possible spirits or deities?
- Possible reference to earth and heavens
- Square - earth
- Circle - heavens

## Context

- Congs were ancient Chinese artifacts found in graves
- Neolithic time period, culture was becoming more settled and agricultural
- The Liangzhu culture was well known for its jadework.
- Depending on size and complexity of the design, and due to the difficulty in working with jade, the process could take months and be very expensive
- Often accompanied by discs called Bi (sounds like B)
