# 24 — Last judgment of Hunefer, from his tomb (page from the Book of the Dead)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** New Kingdom, 19th Dynasty, Egypt. 
**Date:** c. 1275 BCE
**Medium:** Painted papyrus scroll
**Location:** Thebes, Egypt

## Form

- Painted/written on papyrus scroll
- Our images is roughly 15” x 36” in size, but the entire scroll is approximately 39” x 216” (18’)
- Hieroglyphics and colorful illustrations
- Stable composition using strong horizontal lines contrasted with verticals
- Composition divided into registers
- Style is close to Old Kingdom even though it was made during the New Kingdom
  - Rigid figures drawn in composite view (that is, heads and legs in profile with torso from front)

## Function

- Book of spells meant to help the dead navigate the afterlife, avoiding attacks by serpents, crocodiles, insects, gods and more.

## Content

- Narrative of Hunefer in the afterlife undergoing judgment and seeking approval from the gods
- The top left edge of the scroll shows the story starting. Hu-Nefer clothed in white, crouches before a line of deities and presents offerings to show moral goodness on earth.
- Next scene shows Anubis, jackal headed god of embalming leading Hu-Nefer to the Hall of Truth.
- Anubis is scene adjusting scale which balances Hu-Nefers heart on left side and feather of Ma’at on the right side. (Ma’at is god of mortality and order)
- Heavy heart = wickedness and if it weighs more than the feather, that  individual is condemned to non-existence and devoured by Ammit. Ammit is the part-crocodile, part-lion, part hippo next to Anubis.
- Ancient Egyptians believe heart was the seat of emotions, intellect, and character so represented good or bad aspects of a person's life
- Hu-Nefer's heart is lighter than feather so the ethical life he lived can now continue to the afterlife.
- Thoth, god with Ibis head, is on the right of the scale recording the verdict.
- Fourth scene, Hu-Nefer is introduced to Osiris (god of underworld) by falcon-headed son Horus.
- Osiris is on the throne and his wife and sister-in-law (who is a guardian of the afterlife) are behind him
- Throughout this work symbols of eternal life are present in multiple spaces.
  - Ankh anubis and horus hold in left hand
  - Lotus blossom in front of osiris
  - Ostrich feather heald by horus falcon

## Context

- These “books” were written by expert scribes. (it is possible that Hunefer worked on his own book
- Draftsmen were employed to paint these items
- Hunefer was a royal scribe, “Scribe of Divine Offerings” as well as “Overseer of Royal Cattle” which meant he was a person of high status
- There was not one “book of the dead,” rather they were produced for individuals.
  - Wealthier individuals would have them custom made, picking and choosing between spells
  - The not so rich could buy pre-made scrolls with blanks left to insert the individual’s name.
- These scrolls emphasize the significance of spirituality and the afterlife to Egyptians
