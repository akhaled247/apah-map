# 49 — Santa Sabina

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Late Antique Europe. 
**Date:** c. 422–432 CE
**Medium:** Brick and stone, wooden roof
**Location:** Rome, Italy

## Form

- Longitudinal axis
  - Nave with an aisle on either side
  - This is a simple design and is lacking the transept
  - There is an arcade separating the nave from the aisles.
    - The columns are corinthian and have been repurposed from a pagan temple - (example of spolia)
    - Over the arcade there are mosaics representing the representing the Eucharist
    - Other mosaics were higher up the wall, but no longer in existence
- Carved wooden doors
  - Upper left corner - may be the earliest image of crucifixion of Christ
  - Rare example of wooden door this as most don’t last this long
    - These doors have had ample amounts of restoration and many of the panels have been rearranged or they could be missing.
  - Some of the panels vary in style and suggest multiple artists and suggest that they may have been carved at different times.
- Wooden Truss Roof
- Clerestory Windows
  - Selenite (a form of gypsum) instead of glass
- Elaborately carved wood screen

## Function

- Religious/spiritual

## Content

- Arcade has designs representing the Eucharist

## Context

- Located on the Aventine Hill in Rome
- Oldest existing Roman basilica that preserves its original colonnaded rectangular plan and architectural style
- Reuse of the Roman Basilica
- As a basilica, it was associated with the Roman gov’t, lending credibility to the church
- Two Baroque chapels added later
- This church is important as it became a model for many early Christian churches and gives insight into what the original St. Peter’s may have looked like, as it is believed that it drew its inspiration from Old St. Peter’s.
