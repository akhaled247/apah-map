# 82 — Il Gesù, including Triumph of the Name of Jesus ceiling fresco

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Giacomo da Vignola, plan; Giacomo della Porta, facade; Giovanni Battista Gaulli, fresco. 
**Date:** Church: 1568–1584 CE; fresco: 1676–1679 CE
**Medium:** Brick, marble, fresco, and stucco
**Location:** Rome, Italy

## Form

- Plan
  - Single vaulted nave with side chapels, not side aisles
  - Short transept with dome
- Facade
  - Symmetrical
  - Decorative pediments, both triangular and semi-circular
  - Corinthian pilasters
  - Large volute buttresses on each side
  - Niches, bottom two have sculptures
  - Cartouche
    - Bottom one:  IHS (the abbreviation of the Greek form of the name Jesus)
- Fresco
  - Triumph of the Name of Jesus
  - Combination of fresco/stucco
    - Convincing technique of figures moving into the space outside of the composition
    - Ambiguous edges
    - Name of Jesus with cross being the focal point
  - Sfumato
  - Creates a sense of action/drama; the work is theatrical
- The basis of the Society of Jesus was to return to the strictest and most uncompromising obedience to the authority of the church and its ecclesiastical hierarchy.
- It is the main and first Jesuit church in Rome (the “mother church” of the Jesuits), and served as a pattern for other Jesuit churches

## Function

- This is a last judgment scene meant to bring people back to the Catholic Church

## Content

- Tumbling figures, clouds, cast shadows, people from different walks of life

## Context

- Two important components of the counter reformation:
  - Council of Trent
