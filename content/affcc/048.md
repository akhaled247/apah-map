# 48 — Catacomb of Priscilla

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Late Antique Europe. 
**Date:** c. 200–400 CE
**Medium:** Excavated tufa and fresco
**Location:** Rome, Italy

## Form

- Cut from tufa stone under Rome, this particular catacomb is located under a park in Rome.
- There are multilayered galleries, with the oldest on top; in some areas the catacombs are 3 stories deep
- Narrow passageways
- Cubicula - small rooms where wealthier individuals were buried
- Arcosolia -  another type of tomb for the upper classes, often decorated with paintings of religious subjects.
- Loculi - shelves which held bodies
- Inscriptions were written in Greek or Latin on the tombs
- Wall and ceiling paintings that are stylistically influenced by Roman painting, although markedly different in content
- Approximately 8 miles of galleries and 40,000 tombs have been located
- Painted scenes that use foreshortening and resemble Roman style paintings

## Function

- Initially tombs of the Acilius Glabrio family
- burial for Christians

## Content

- Images that illustrate old and new testament stories
  - Themes of hope for resurrection and eternal life
  - The Cubiculum of the Veiled Woman c. 250
    - 3 scenes, same women
      - Marriage
      - Motherhood
      - Resurrection
    - The woman is standing in a pose known as an orant.
    - Good Shepherd - a symbol of Christ
      - In the Gospel of John, Jesus states "I am the good shepherd" in two verses, John 10:11 and 10:14. I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.
    - doves/peacocks
      - Doves representing the Holy Spirit and peace
      - Peacocks represent the resurrection of Christ and eternal life.
      - Both symbols had also held meaning for Greek and Roman mythology.
        - Peacock Roman - Peacocks were a widespread symbol of immortality
        - Peacock Greek - believed that the flesh of a peacock did not decay after death, so they believed the peacock to be a sign of immortality.
        - Dove - Roman - peace
        - Dove - Greek - associated with Aphrodite the goddess of love
    - Jonah and the whale
      - Book of Jonah is in old testament
      - Considered a minor prophet
      - Avoids going to Nineveh, boards ship, tossed overboard, swallowed by large fish where he remains for three days
      - Something as a foreshadowing of Christ’s death and resurrection
    - Sacrifice of Isaac
      - Genesis 21 - 22
      - Abraham demonstrated his obedience to God in his willingness to sacrifice Isaac.
      - This was another foreshadowing of Christ and God’s willingness to sacrifice his son for the sins of man.
    - Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego saved from furnace
      - 3 Jewish slaves in service to the Babylonian king Nebuchadnezzar
      - King set a statue of gold, and the three refused to bow down and were consequently thrown into a furnace.
      - A fourth figure was seen in the furnace with the other three
      - The three were spared and came forth from the furnace unharmed.
  - The Greek Chapel
    - Named for the writing on the wall which was one of the first things discoverers saw. (That is, writings in Greek, but also contains text in Latin)
    - Decorated with paintings done in the first style of Roman wall painting, Incrustation. (refer to 4 styles of Roman painting)
    - 3 niches for sarcophagi and a long bench for funeral banquets
    - Painting depicting funeral banquet or maybe banquet of the Holy Eucharist (or Lord’s Supper)
      - Found recorded in three of the gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke as well as 1 Corinthians
      - Represents the blood and body of Christ
    - A variety of old and new testament scenes including
      - Noah
      - Moses
      - Isaac
      - 3 stories from Daniel
      - Miracles of healing and resurrection from new testament
      - Adoration of the Magi
    - While the subjects may be familiar, the way in which they are depicted differs from more contemporary portrayals.
      - This would seem to indicate that what is visible is the development of Christian iconography.

## Context

- This is one of many catacombs in Rome
- It was dug between the 2nd - 5th centuries
- It was located at the villa of a wealthy woman named Priscilla
  - She donated the land where her family would be buried, and later the Christian community would as well.
- Wealthy individuals would have a large area carved out that would hold a sarcophagus (a cubicula), where most individuals would have a shelf (a Loculi).
- The tombs are now empty, at one time there was most likely graverobbing, both for material items, but also relics.
- The Roman empire was under the control of Trajan, Hadrian, Dioclentian, Constantine, Theodosius
  - In 313 CE, Constantine signed the Edict of MIlan, after which Christianity was tolerated.
  - Persecution of Christians in late 200’s (Diocletian)
  - Gaining acceptance of Christianity during 300’s
- Throughout this time period, there was division/reunification/division of the Roman Empire
- Not really any record of Christian art prior to the second century.
