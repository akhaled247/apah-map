# 12 — White Temple and its ziggurat

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Sumerian. 
**Date:** c. 3500–3000 BCE
**Medium:** Mud brick
**Location:** Uruk (modern Warka, Iraq)

## Form

- Ziggurat - it is a mound shaped similar to a pyramid with the top cut off.
  - Made from mud bricks serving as a base for the temple
  - Total height of Z & T is approximately 40’
  - Flat top was bitumen covered with brick to serve as foundation for the temple
- Temple - a structure built for the purpose of religious activities
  - Tripartite design
    - Rectangular central hall flanked by rooms on either side
    - Three entrances - none faced ramp, required
  - Corners were aligned to the cardinal directions
  - Whitewashed inside and out

## Function

- Religious - Temples was dedicated to deities
- The ziggurat + tower could be seen high above the city as a symbol of power to those approaching
- Heart of the theocratic political system
  - Theocracy is a government where a god is ruler, and state officials work on his behalf.
  - A ziggurat is a visual connection to the god/goddess honored there, and the deity’s political authority.
- Z & T’s Symbolized bridges between earth and heavens.
- Meeting place for humans and their gods.

## Content

- This Z & T was dedicated to the sky god Anu
  - Anu was believed to be the supreme source of all authority, for the other gods and for all mortal rulers
  - Ritually buried bones of a leopard and a lion

## Context

- Ziggurats were common to Sumerians, Babylonians and Assyrians
- Sumerians built before Egyptians built pyramids, though they were less impressive than pyramids
- Quite likely that slavery was used for the construction
- Uruk
  - City life began more than 5,000 years ago
  - Area where writing emerged
  - Great importance to southern Mesopotamia
  - Uruk was initially ruled by a priest-king, and it is believed that many early Mesopotamian cities followed the same model
