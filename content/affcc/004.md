# 4 — Running horned woman

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Neolithic Sahara. 
**Date:** 6000–4000 BCE
**Medium:** Pigment on rock
**Location:** Tassili n'Ajjer, Algeria

## Form

- Rock painting - AP image is enhanced to make it more visible. Actual work is difficult to make out because of the degradation of the pigments.
- Very little of the colors remain. Painted with pigments suspended in water.
- Stylized female appears to be running
- Use of hierarchical scale, with multiple smaller figures surrounding larger woman
- The horns use twisted perspective similar to other cave paintings (this is also referred to as composite pose)

## Function

- Unknown, could represent a ritualistic ceremony.

## Content

- Woman possibly wearing costume for ritualistic dance
- Patterning with dots prevalent
- Hands, knees, ankles, with tassels
- Headdress? Dots raining or dangling down.

## Context

- Located in Sahara Desert of northern Africa, neolithic
- Area has high concentration of cave paintings
- Lieutenant Brenans of the French Foreign Legion found the paintings, executed sketches between 1933 - 1940\.
- Brenans was not the first to discover, had been visited by
- Europeans previously.
- Indiginous peoples already knew the sites and served as guides.
- Archaeologist Henri Lhote made many visits to the site and wrote a book that is still today an important text on archaeology.
- Works are in danger today from
- years of wetting surfaces to make the drawings show up better.
- Vandalism
- Theft
