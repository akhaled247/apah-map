# 15 — Seated scribe

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Old Kingdom, 4th Dynasty, Egypt. 
**Date:** c. 2620–2500 BCE
**Medium:** Painted limestone
**Location:** Saqqara, Egypt

## Form

- This is a painted limestone sculpture with eyes made from minerals and wooden nipples
- Seated, frontal facing, legs crossed attentive, weaning a kilt and ready to write
- Hands in writing position and holding a papyrus. The right hand is missing the reed brush
- Slightly pudgy body

## Function

- This sculpture has a frontal appearance and is most likely meant for placement in a tomb
- Possible this was to serve as dwelling for scribes “ka” in afterlife
- A ka statue is a type of ancient Egyptian statue intended to provide a resting place for the ka (life-force or spirit) of the person after death.
- This sculpture may provide a scribe for pharaoh in afterlife, since it was possibly placed in pharaoh’s tomb

## Content

- His body suggests that he doesn’t perform manual labor
- Crossed legs would have been typical posture for a scribe
- This informal pose and natural representation would have never been used for a pharaoh

## Context

- Originally discovered in 1850 by the French archaeologist Auguste Mariette in a necropolis in Saqqara (sa-car-uh)
- The exact location of the find is unknown, as Mariette’s records have been lost
- Scribes were highly regarded as most of the population was unable to read or write
