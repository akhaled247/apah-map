# 23 — Tutankhamun's tomb, innermost coffin

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** New Kingdom, 18th Dynasty, Egypt. 
**Date:** c. 1323 BCE
**Medium:** Gold with inlay of enamel and semiprecious stones
**Location:** Valley of the Kings, Egypt

## Form

- Solid gold coffin weighing 243 lbs. embellished with Inlaid with semi precious stones such as lapis lazuli, turquoise, and carnelian
- Anthropoid (resembling a human) coffin
- Nemes, fake beard, cobra and vulture, crook and flail
- Patterning throughout, hieroglyphics down front from waist to toes

## Function

- Nesting of coffins showed an elite status of the individual
- It was also intended to ensure the transformation of the deceased from human to deity by connecting the deceased their ancestors, to Osiris, the god of the afterlife, and to Amun-Ra, the sun- and creator god

## Content

- A mummified body found in the innermost of 3 nested coffins which were placed inside of a stone sarcophagus.
- Tutanhhamun is shown here in his divine form in the afterlife (the gods were thought to have skin of gold). He holds the crook and flail, symbols of the king's right to rule.
- The crook and flail were symbols used in ancient Egyptian society. They were originally the attributes of the deity Osiris that became insignia of pharaonic authority.
- The cobra was a symbol of Lower Egypt (the goddess Wadjet) and the vulture a symbols of upper Egypt (the goddess Nekhbet), both were symbols of royal protection

## Context

- Dad was Pharaoh Akhenaton who reformed Egypt and changed religion from polytheistic to monotheistic and was responsible for the Amarna style in Egyptian art.
- Only significant thing about Tutankhaman’s 10 year reign is that he abandoned his father Akhenaten's monotheism and reestablished old polytheistic religion and priests.
- As part of this moves capital from Amarna back to Thebes. (where temple of Amun-Re is)
- Tut became pharaoh as a boy and died at the age of 18 from reasons unknown
- A British archaeologist named Howard Carter discovered Tut’s tomb in 1922
- This gave the world insight to the burial of Egyptian pharaohs because it was mostly intact and undisturbed.
