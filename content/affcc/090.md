# 90 — Angel with Arquebus, Asiel Timor Dei

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Master of Calamarca (José López de los Ríos). 
**Date:** c. 17th century CE
**Medium:** Oil on canvas
**Location:** Calamarca, Bolivia

## Form

- Oil on canvas
- Dimensions: approximately 63” x 43”
- Dark ground, ornately dressed in angel holding an early type of long gun.

## Function

- Representing celestial, aristocratic, and military beings all at once, these angels were created after the first missionizing period, as Christian missionary orders persistently sought to terminate the practice of pre-Hispanic religions and enforce Catholicism.

## Content

- Angel with firearm
- The dress corresponds to the dress of Andean aristocrats and Inka royalty. The dress of Asiel Timor Dei was an Andean invention that combined contemporary European fashion and the typical dress of indigenous noblemen.

## Context

- harquebus, also spelled arquebus, also called hackbut, first gun fired from the shoulder, a smoothbore matchlock with a stock resembling that of a rifle. The harquebus was invented in Spain in the mid-15th century.
- These types of paintings were produced from the late seventeenth century through the nineteenth century in the viceroyalty of Peru
- Guns were a symbol of power/dominance over native American people and their beliefs
