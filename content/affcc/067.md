# 67 — Pazzi Chapel

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Filippo Brunelleschi (architect). 
**Date:** c. 1429–1461 CE
**Medium:** Masonry (pietra serena and masonry)
**Location:** Basilica di Santa Croce, Florence, Italy

## Form

- Renaissance style
- Geometric shapes
  - Rectangles
  - Squares
  - Circles
- Repetition of shapes
- Facade
  - Loggia resembling a Roman triumphal arch
  - It was created using pietra serena, a gray sandstone that is soft and tends to crumble over time.
  - Slender Corinthian columns
  - Pilasters
  - Arched windows
  - Small pediment over the portal
- Interior feel of Roman architecture
- Pietra Serena stone (greenish gray)
- Similar to a centrally planned interior
  - Is rectangular
  - Short barrel vaults to either end of the dome
- Dome resting on pendentives
- Small tholos atop the dome
- Oculus
- Glazed ceramics roundels on the pendentives

## Function

- Meeting room/classroom for monks
- Chapel was to function as a burial place for his family

## Context

- Revival of classicism
- Some famous architects were  Filippo Brunelleschi, Leon Battista Alberti, Donato Bramante, Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo and Andrea Palladio
- Brunelleschi was architect
  - Considered to be a founding father of Renaissance architecture
  - Completed after the death of Brunelleschi
  - designed the dome of the Florence Cathedral
  - Developed linear perspective
- Rich Florentine banker Andrea de' Pazzi, wealth was second only to the Medici family
- More interesting than the chapel is the Pazzi conspiracy of April 26, 1478.
