# 70 — Palazzo Rucellai

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Leon Battista Alberti (architect). 
**Date:** c. 1450 CE
**Medium:** Stone, masonry
**Location:** Florence, Italy

## Form

- Renaissance
- Revival of Roman architecture
- Near Symmetrical facade, strong use of repetition
- Divided into 3 stories
  - stories of the Rucellai facade have different classical orders, as in the Colosseum
  - but with the Tuscan order at the base
  - a Renaissance original in place of the Ionic order at the second level
  - and a very simplified Corinthian order at the top level.
- Pilasters
- Arches above windows, except bottom floor
- Elements become lighter as they move upward
- Trabeated (tra-be-a-ted) doorways (post and lintel)
- Faux opus reticulatum

## Function

- palace

## Content

- Design based on writings of Vitruvius, vocabulary, proportions, harmonies
- Frieze on second floor (wind filled sail) is a historical family reference
- Family coat of armour over 2 of the second story windows

## Context

- Patron: Giovanni di Paolo Rucellai (1403–1481)
  - member of a wealthy family of wool merchants
  - During Renaissance, in Florence (City), in Tuscany (region), Italy
  - Rucellai worked for the Medici family
- Medici Palace was built roughly same time and in close proximity to the Rucellai Palazzo
- Architect was Alberti
- Looking to Vitruvius
  - Individual who wrote most important documents on Roman architecture.
  - Through his writings we have a better understanding of Etruscan Temples
- Palazzo Rucellai, influenced by the colosseum in Rome, in turn, influenced such later buildings as the Palazzo della Cancelleria (later the Papal Chancery, in Rome).
