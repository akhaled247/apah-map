# 19 — The Code of Hammurabi

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Babylonian (Susian). 
**Date:** c. 1792–1750 BCE
**Medium:** Basalt
**Location:** Babylon (found at Susa, modern Iran)

## Form

- Stele, almost 7’ in height, with carved relief at top
- The writing is engraved cuneiform in the Akkadian language
- Composite views - front view of torso, profile of rest of body
- Sun god at top shows his divine nature by the horned crown and rays that emanate from his shoulders

## Function

- Elevation of Hammurabi to near deity status
- Records laws for equal treatment of people

## Content

- Pictured at the top is Hammurabi with Shamash, the Babylonian sun god and god of justice.
  - Hammurabi is receiving ring and scepter from the sun god demonstrating the divine nature of the law and the god’s approval of Hammurabi
- Record of laws
  - Probably not written by Hammurabi, but legal precedents
  - Laws pertaining to everyday life, written in common language

## Context

- Captured in the 12th century BCE and moved from Sippar to Susa, where it was discovered in 1901 and moved to the Louvre Museum
- Historians believe the Elamite King Shutruk-Nahhunte plundered the four-ton slab during a 12th century B.C. raid on the Babylonian city of Sippar
