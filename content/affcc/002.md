# 2 — Great Hall of the Bulls

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Upper Paleolithic Europe. 
**Date:** 15,000–13,000 BCE
**Medium:** Rock painting (pigment on limestone)
**Location:** Lascaux, France

## Form

- Large in scale (largest bull is 11’6” long)
- Some silhouetted with color, others outlined
- Images are usually drawn in a manner to best represent the animal, profile with all four legs seen and if applicable, horns in twisted perspective.
- Use of twisted perspective - animal from side, but horns from a frontal view. You may also find this referred to as a composite view.
- Drawn, painted, blown materials create the artworks
- Overlapping shapes could be an indication of various artists working over a period of time or possibly showing an awareness of naturalism depicting some animals in front of others.
- Varying sizes

## Function

- No certain meaning, variety of theories, but most have been discredited over time
- Ritualistic,hunting magic?
- The location of the artwork within the caves indicates a possible ritualistic function as many are far from the cave’s entrance.
- It has been suggested that there was a connection between the  mortal and spiritual worlds that was made through the act of painting.

## Content

- Representational, yet non-natural images of animals
- Variety of recognizable animals give indication of wildlife present
- Overlapping of forms can give a feeling of naturalism and depth

## Context

- Located at a cave in Lascaux France
- Discovered in 1940
- Caves closed to public, but nearby historical center has replications
- Similar to some 350 other caves, mostly in southern France and northern Spain
- Most paintings hundreds of feet from cave openings
- Holes in walls might have held scaffolding
- Animal fat lamps found
- Theory - Breuil, used ethnography - hunting magic
