# 98 — The Tête à Tête, from Marriage à la Mode

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** William Hogarth. 
**Date:** c. 1743 CE
**Medium:** Oil on canvas
**Location:** London, United Kingdom

## Form

- Husband, wife, accountant
- Symbolic paintings and nicknacks
- Gilded frames religious paintings
- Painting with nude foot on bed, with the rest of the painting covered by a curtain
- Signs of sexually transmitted diseases

## Content

- The images in this set of 6 are targeted to wealthy middle class; makes fun of the upper levels of society
- Addresses the concern over marriages arranged for economic reasons, not love.
- The story:
  - Characters: the Sqanderfield family, father and son - has a title but without money; the wealthy merchant and his daughter; Silvertongue (offers counsel, talks girl into the marriage)
  - Lord Sanderfield has his son marry a the daughter of a wealthy merchant
  - The daughter, in return for the marriage, get the aristocratic title.

## Context

- Hogarth is best known for his series paintings of 'modern moral subjects', of which he sold engravings on subscription.
- His engravings were so plagiarized that he lobbied for the Copyright Act of 1735 as protection for writers and artists.
- The earliest beginnings of the industrial revolution are starting in England and France and with that, a growing middle class. The aristocracy is losing power to the wealthy merchant class.
- Art, especially prints, are becoming a commodity.
