# 56 — Great Mosque of Córdoba

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Umayyad Spain. 
**Date:** c. 785–786 CE
**Medium:** Stone masonry
**Location:** Córdoba, Spain

## Form

- Mosaics
- hypostyle prayer hall
  - built with recycled ancient Roman columns from which sprout a striking combination of two-tiered, symmetrical arches, formed of stone and red brick.
  - MIhrab (mir rub) - A mihrab is used in a mosque to identify the wall that faces Mecca—the birth place of Islam
  - These arches illustrate a type of arch known as Horseshoe style. They spread across North Africa from Morocco to Egypt
- a courtyard with a fountain in the middle
- an orange grove, fruit common in Damascu, Syria
- a covered walkway circling the courtyard
- a minaret (a tower used to call the faithful to prayer) that is now encased in a squared, tapered bell tower
- The dome is built of crisscrossing ribs that create pointed arches all lavishly covered with gold mosaic in a radial pattern. This building technique anticipates later Gothic rib vaulting, though on a more modest scale.

## Function

- Church
- Mosque
- Church

## Content

- Mosaics found in excavations of the earlier basilica continue the Roman and Paleo-Christian tradition, differentiating between three decorative areas. The first is an ornamentation of intersecting arches. In the second we find a crater with flowers next to a dove and a crown of thorns, referring to the Holy Spirit and martyrdom. Third, occupying almost the entire surface area, we find a succession of geometric motifs and symbols such as the cross.

## Context

- Possible site of temple honoring Roman god Janus.
- Built on site of San Vicente Basilica (Visigoth, from mid 6th century)
  - Visigoths, the people that ruled this area after the Roman empire collapsed and before the Umayyads arrived.
- Started in 786, the building was expanded over a period of two hundred years.
- Converted into a mosque and then completely rebuilt by the descendants of the exiled Umayyads—the first Islamic dynasty who had originally ruled from their capital Damascus (in present-day Syria) from 661 until 750.
- Back to a cathedral
- Various expansions throughout the centuries
