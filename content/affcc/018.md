# 18 — King Menkaura and queen

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Old Kingdom, 4th Dynasty, Egypt. 
**Date:** c. 2490–2472 BCE
**Medium:** Greywacke
**Location:** Giza, Egypt

## Form

- Dimensions: 56 x 22 1/2 x 21 3/4 in.
- Stiff/rigid forms, arms at side, left foot forward
- Stylized/Idealized bodies
- King is wearing nemes and a false beard and wrap around kilt, all symbols of his position.
- Queen wearing a tight, sheer dress
- Traces of paint remain
- The queen’s foot is also slightly forward, uncommon for Egyptian sculptures
- Unusual for a pharaoh’s image, the king has no protective cobra (known as a uraeus) perched on his brow. This absence suggests that both the king’s nemes and the queen’s wig were originally covered in a sheath of precious metal and that the cobra would have been part of that addition.
- The slab behind that joins the two forms, giving strength to the sculpture

## Function

- This is a funerary item as it was found in Kenkaura’s valley temple
- Some suggest that it was meant to house the “ka”

## Content

- The base is left unfinished and uninscribed. This is usually where the names of the individuals are placed. It is assumed that it is King Menkaura as it was found in Menkaura's Valley Temple and because it resembles other statues from the same findspot bearing his name.
- The name of the women is unknown, Menkaura had 3 wives. Some have suggested that it was the goddess Hathor, but she exhibits no divine qualities. Some think it may have been his mother, as some later kings were shown with their mothers. But, in private sculptures where a man and woman are shown, and names indicated, it is usually husband and wife.

## Context

- Pharaohs wore false beards that were often passed down. It was an association with the god Osiris and a symbol of their rise to divine status. Both men and women pharaohs wore them
- Valley Temples were used to perpetuate the cult of the deceased king and were active places of worship for hundreds of years (sometimes much longer) after the king’s death.
