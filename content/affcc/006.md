# 6 — Anthropomorphic stele

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Prehistoric Arabian Peninsula. 
**Date:** Fourth millennium BCE
**Medium:** Sandstone
**Location:** Near Ha'il, Saudi Arabia

## Form

- Vertical stone, similar to a headstone, approx 3’ high
- Simplified, stylized human representation done in relief
- Can be viewed in round, but with emphasis on front
- Stone texture and color utilized in the final work

## Function

- Suspected use is as a grave marker (a stele)

## Content

- Abstract male figure, close set eyes,
- Robe, belt, dagger, diagonal cords crossing torse with an awl

## Context

- Region was not arid, but rather rich in vegetation during the time this was created
- People were nomadic, but were beginning to settle. Moving from hunting to agriculture
- Settlements were often occupied seasonally
- This meant there was travel between distant regions
- Found in a small village near Ha’il in northwest Saudi Arabia
- Shares characteristics with steles far from this site
- Demonstrates use of the human form prior to the rise of Islam
- Humans are mostly absent from Islam work (most work focuses on geometric designs/patterns)
