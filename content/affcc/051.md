# 51 — San Vitale

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Early Byzantine Europe. 
**Date:** c. 526–547 CE
**Medium:** Brick, marble, and stone veneer; mosaic
**Location:** Ravenna, Italy

## Form

- Central plan, octagon
- Ambulatory
- Extension with apse at end
- Brick veneer - reused from earlier Roman Bldg.
- Early example of flying buttresses
- Apse mosaic [https://www.christianiconography.info/Wikimedia%20Commons/apseSanVitale.html](https://www.christianiconography.info/Wikimedia%20Commons/apseSanVitale.html)
  - Christ sitting on an orb ( represents earth)
  - Angels
  - Book of the apocalypse (scroll with seven seals)
  - Martyr of city
    - On our left, the right hand of Christ
    - Receiving a crown from Christ
  - Founder of building
    - On our right, the left hand of Christ
    - Shown presenting the church to Christ
  - Other religious symbolism
    - White Lilies - Christ’s purity/divinity
    - Peacocks - symbol of rebirth
- Columns that don’t belong to the recognized orders -
  - New invention of Christian architecture
    - Impost blocks
- Other mosaics
  - Style has left the classical, these are distinctively medieval
  - Justinian
    - Justinian control over Italy, even in Constantinople
    - Symbolism of church, government and military
    - Negative space filled with gold - Byzantine characteristic
  - Theodora
    - Justinian’s bride
    - Mirrors Justinian's panel

## Function

- House of religious worship
- Convey message of Justinian authority

## Content

- Mosaics - Political leadership of Justinian
  - Defender of the faith (Orthodox Christianity)
  - Rebuilder of Roman Empire
  - Justinian carrying bread for Eucharist
  - Religious Leaders
    - Cross
    - Gospel book
    - Censer (incense burner)
  - Political leaders on left (note purple stripe)
  - Military far left

## Context

- Ravenna was under Justinian control at this point, though he probably never went there.
- Byzantine architecture flourished under Justinian
  - Characterized by large domes and interior mosaics
  - Style spanned for centuries and continues to be an architectural influence today
  - Much of the architecture is related to the church
  - Churches that were destroyed (fire, riots, etc) often had new structures built upon the rubble of the old.
  - Original Greek churches were built using a central plan(similar to the Greek cross as opposed to the Latin cross plans
