# 52 — Hagia Sophia

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus. 
**Date:** 532–537 CE
**Medium:** Brick and ceramic elements with stone and mosaic veneer
**Location:** Constantinople (Istanbul, Turkey)

## Form

- Combines longitudinal plan of a basilica and a central plan in one building
- 105’ main dome supported by pendentives and two semi-domes
- 3 Aisles separated by columns, galleries above
- Marble piers helping to support the dome
- Decorative capital atop the columns appear too delicate to support the function
  - They are a derivative of the Roman ionic and the Byzantine original inventions
  - Called a basket capital
  - Intricate carving creates dark shadows and gives the appearance of filigree work
- Dome has closely spaced windows that give a light airy feel to the structure
  - Light strikes gold mosaics the line the windows

## Function

- Church
- Mosque
- Museum
- Mosque (again)

## Content

- Mosaics
  - Christian symbolism
  - Not a great deal of imagery representing the human form
  - Islamic inscriptions in the dome as a result of the conversion to a mosque
- Marble, precious stones - materials that Justinian imported from throughout the empire.

## Context

- Building reflects changes that have occurred within the region throughout the years
- Built in about 6 years, unusual for its time
- Built on location of two previous buildings, both destroyed fire by during rioting
- It was looted during the crusades
- Became a mosque after the Turkish conquest of Constantinople
- 1935 converted to a museum and in 2020 it was converted back to a mosque
- Loss of relics and other Christian items as a result of the crusades and the sacking of Constantinople
