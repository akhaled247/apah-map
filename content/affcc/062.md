# 62 — Röttgen Pietà

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Late medieval Europe (German). 
**Date:** c. 1300–1325 CE
**Medium:** Painted wood
**Location:** Rhineland, Germany

## Form

- Materials - wood and paint
- Color brings human qualities to Mary; suffering of Christ
- Stigmata
- Thin and frail body of Christ
- Repetition of rosettes at the bottom of the sculpture (3)
- Significance of Red and Green?
- Heavy drapery
- Sharp thorns
- Opposing of angles create tension
- Grotesque forms/proportions

## Function

- Used as a devotional aid
- Create an emotional response on part of viewer
- A new style of “Christ”, one who demonstrates his human quality and is able to identify with viewers. This is in contrast to the Triumphant Christ or Patient Christ, who was depicted as being immune to human suffering.

## Content

- Mary/Christ
- Mary’s suffering and human nature
  - Mary’s facial expression/response is unique from other pieta sculptures created during the period
  - Mary shows anger, heaviness, defeat
- Christ’s suffering and human nature
- God/Christ understand human suffering
- Isolation of forms de-emphasizes the narrative and emphasizes the emotion

## Context

- Sculpture is an additive (hand-building with clay or wax) or subtractive process (such as carving from stone or wood)
- More than half of the sculpture in medieval Europe was from wood.
- A Pieta is an Italian word for a work of art where Mary is holding the body of her dead son, Jesus after his crucifixion on the cross. It is an example of a lamentation scene.
- Possibly from a nunnery
- Current location: Rheinisches Landesmuseum in Bonn
