# 54 — Virgin (Theotokos) and Child between Saints Theodore and George

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Early Byzantine Europe. 
**Date:** Sixth or early seventh century CE
**Medium:** Encaustic on wood
**Location:** Monastery of Saint Catherine, Mount Sinai, Egypt

## Form

- Encaustic on wood
  - [https://www.widewalls.ch/magazine/encaustic-ancient-painting-technique](https://www.widewalls.ch/magazine/encaustic-ancient-painting-technique)
  - Adding pigment and other chemicals to hot wax which is applied to the support
  - Used as far back as the ancient egyptians and greeks
- Relatively small in scale: 2′ 3″ x 1′ 7 3/8″
- Symmetrical composition
- Implied Cross
- Some modeling of form that hearkens back to classicism.
- Unnatural proportions
  - Little feet, little hands
  - Large eyes, not properly located in head
  - Triangular heads
- Shallow space, yet with some indication of depth
  - Architectural elements at top left and right
- Ornate attire
- Nimbus: a luminous cloud or a halo surrounding a supernatural being or a saint.

## Function

- Personal, intimate medium for spiritual transactions with holy figures
- Some believed them to be wonder working and associated miracles with them.

## Content

- Virgin and Child flanked by two soldier saints, St. Theodore to the left and St. George to the right. Above these are two angels who gaze upward to the hand of God, from which light emanates, falling on the Virgin.

## Context

- 500’s - 600’s
- Egyptian Monastery
- Not everyone approved of these icons, citing the old testament commandment against graven images
- The eight century saw strong opposition to these icons and many were destroyed. Some in remote regions`
