# 41 — Seated boxer (Boxer at Rest)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Hellenistic Greek. 
**Date:** c. 100 BCE
**Medium:** Bronze with copper inlays
**Location:** Rome, Italy (found on Quirinal Hill; Greek origin)

## Form

- Natural, non-idealized figure
- Made from bronze using a lost wax process
- The work is hollow with a thin metal shell
- ’2” in height
- Inlays of copper represent a bloodied figure

## Function

- It possibly commemorates an actual boxer

## Content

- Traditional subject of an athlete, but depicted in a non-traditional way.
- Beaten, exhausted, cauliflower ear, hands wrapped
- Older athlete
- His pose and the treatment of his beard recall two statues of Herakles

## Context

- During the Hellenistic period, there was movement away from the idealized form, to extreme naturalism.
- This opened the door for a new range of subjects
- Old subjects were also executed, but with a new
- Found at Quirinal Hill of Rome in 1885
- Not many bronzes remain as they were melted down for their metal.
- Was an actual Greek sculpture, not a Roman copy.
