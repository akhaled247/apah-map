# 89 — Ecstasy of Saint Teresa

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Gian Lorenzo Bernini. 
**Date:** 1647–1652 CE
**Medium:** Marble (sculpture); stucco and gilt bronze (chapel)
**Location:** Cornaro Chapel, Santa Maria della Vittoria, Rome, Italy

## Form

- Baroque facade
- Colored marble interior
- Stained glass inside
- Gilding
- Sculpture is marble and uses supports from below to allow strong shadows to give a sense that it is floating
- Angel’s clothing is light and flowing in contrast to the heavy folds of Teresa’s garment
- There is a broken pediment above the sculpture creating a theater like space
  - Behind the pediment is a window that is hidden from view
  - It illuminates the golden rods as though the Holy Spirit is shining down on the scene.
- The patron and other viewers are sculpted into niches on either side of the sculpture (Frederico Cornaro, a Venice cardinal)

## Function

- Attempting to bring viewer back to the Catholic faith

## Content

- St Teresa sculpture depicting one of her angelic visions
  - She had been recently canonized (1622)
  - Bernini used one of Teresa’s writings as the inspiration for the sculpture

## Context

- Deeply religious individual
- Very interested in theater
