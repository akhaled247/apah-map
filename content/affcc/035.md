# 35 — Acropolis (Parthenon, Helios, Plaque of the Ergastines, Temple of Athena Nike, Victory adjusting her sandal)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Iktinos and Kallikrates (architects); Phidias (sculptor). 
**Date:** c. 447–410 BCE
**Medium:** Marble
**Location:** Athens, Greece

## Form

- an ancient citadel located on a rocky outcrop above the city of Athens
- Is approximately 500’ above Athens and approximately 7 ½ acres (more than 5 ½ football fields
- This is a Doric temple with Ionic elements
  - Broad, fluted Doric columns that rest directly upon the temple floor (the stylobate)
  - Inside the west end of the temple, there were 4 Ionic columns
  - There is a frieze (an Ionic element) located along the top outside edge of the cella.
- The plan is peripteral -  meaning that it has columns on each side. It is also octastyle meaning there are eight column across the front
- The columns were built by stacking a series of “drums” and not monolithic columns.
- On the east end of the temple was the entrance, although people would be approaching the temple from the west.
  - Sculpture adorned all 4 sides in the metopes and on the pediment of both the east and west ends of the building
  - North
    - Metopes
      - The thirty two metopes represent the Fall of Troy to the allied armies of the Greek kings.
      - The reason for the Greeks' campaign against Troy in Asia Minor was the abduction of Helen, wife of the Spartan king Menelaos, by Paris, prince of Troy.
    - Frieze
      - The frieze on the north side of the Parthenon depicts part of the procession formed by the people of Athens during the Panathenaic festival.
  - South
    - Metopes
      - Currently in the British Museum, the s. Metopes show the battle between Centaurs and Lapiths at the marriage feast of Peirithoos. (pie-rith-or-us)
    - Frieze
      - Panathenaic procession - Ten groups of six horsemen, defined by dress, probably represent the division of the Athenian cavalry itself into ten tribal units
  - East
    - Metopes
      - There are fourteen metopes that represent the Gigantomachy, the battle between the Olympian gods and the Giants, in which goddess Athena played a leading role
    - Frieze
      - The east side of the Parthenon frieze is its culmination
      - The processions on the other sides converge.
      - At the center sit the gods seated in two groups, facing each approaching stream of participants.
    - Pediment
      - The east pediment represented the birth of Athena, fully grown, ready for battle, born from the head of Zeus
  - West
    - Metopes
      - depicts the Amazonomachy, the struggle of Athenian youths and their king Theseus against the Amazons.
      - The Amazons, a mythical tribe of women-warriors from the Black Sea, invaded Athens threatening even the Acropolis.
      - The reason for the invasion was the kidnapping of their queen Antiope by Theseus.
    - Frieze
      - shows the riders preparing to take part in the procession of the Athenian people during the Panathenaic festival
    - Pediment
      - The west pediment faced the Propylaia and depicted the contest between Athena and Poseidon during their competition for the honor of becoming the city’s patron
  - The pediments, metopes and the friezes would have originally been brightly painted.
- While the building is designed around mathematical proportions in search of perfection, there are subtle deviations from those ratios that aid in the illusion of  perfection.
- There was at one point a 36’ tall statue of Athena located within the temple.
- Triangular shaped sculpture that filled the east pediment of the Parthenon
- Meant to be read from left to right
- Today, it contains remnants of characters who would have been present for the birth of Athena from Zeus’ head.
  - Both of the central figures are no longer in existence.
  - On the far left is Helios and his horses representing the rising sun.
  - The god of wine, Dionysis, slightly reclining with hand holding something (now missing)
    - Obvious attention is given to the male anatomy and careful rendering of the form.
  - Two  figures to the left of center seem to be aware of what has happened and are reacting in surprise.
  - On the right there is a group of three goddesses.
    - These figures demonstrate an example of the Phidian style of sculpting that is non-natural while appearing to be so; it is rather exaggerated, to reveal the human form beneath the fabric.
  - On the extreme right, there is a horse’s head that represents the moon.
- Amphiprostyle
  - There are 4 columns on each end, with none on the sides.
  - Ionian style - note the capitals are shaped like scrolls (called Volutes) and there is a frieze around the top perimeter of the structure
- It is also a tetrastyle, having 4 columns on the backside of the temple; as this is the side facing the entrance of the Acropolis.
- The columns are monolithic, meaning carved from a single block of stone
- It is the smallest of the Athenian acropolis temples and is located next to the Propylaia.
- A marble relief sculpture
- The height is 3’6”
- Phidias style sculpture
- Fairly high relief carvings face the outside edge of the parapet and would be visible to those approaching the Acropolis
- While the figure is maintaining a pose that is off-balance, there is a sense of stability given by the softly curved main line that runs from the head through the torso and on down to Athena’s left leg in near vertical fashion.
- Following the right knee through the lower body leads one to the position of the left hand. (continuation and stability provided by the horizontal implied line)
- The intersection of these two implied lines is near the center of the composition.
- Athena’s right leg is raised with the thigh nearly horizontal. (stable)
- There are a series of gentle flowing curves repeated throughout the composition. Nike’s upper left wing, next to the left arm, and again running from the neck, through the torso and down to the left knee.
- These lines are contrasted by the opposing drapery folds that flow from Athena’s right knee downward and to the right.
- Marble relief sculpture
- Slightly more than 3’ in height
- Phidias style sculpture
- There is near symmetry created by grouping and placement of the people within the composition.
- Depth is suggested by changes in the amount of relief given to the forms.
- A vertical rhythm is created by the repetition of the figures, yet it refrains from being static by the opposition of the male figures.
  - vertical lines are emphasized in the clothing of the women.
- Originally, it would have been brightly painted

## Function

- Throughout history, its purpose has changed. It served as a home for a king, a fortress, a treasury, a religious center, tourist attraction
- Acted as a treasury, but  dedicated to the goddess Athena
- Greek temple>Greek Othodox Church>Roman Catholic Church>mosque
- Conveys a mythological narrative - the birth of Athena
- The temple was well placed for defense, but also served as a place of worship to the goddess of victory.
- The parapet itself was to prevent individuals from falling off of the bastion that the temple was built upon
- The relief sculptures were visible decorations to those approaching the acropolis
- These images elevated the people of Athens to near god-like status as temples usually depicted only deities and mythological events.
- The frieze was a narrative that most likely portrayed the Panathenaic Procession

## Content

- There are 4 structures still standing:
  - the Parthenon
  - the Temple of Athena Nike
  - the Erechtheion
  - the The Propylaea
- Some structures and sculptures are no longer in existence and some had new structures built on top of the old.
- Mythological story of Poseidon and Athena vying to become the patron deity of the city
  - Poseidon - god of sea
  - Athena - goddess of wisdom, war
  - Both gods give a gift
    - Poseidon - naval superiority
    - Athena - prosperity and peace
  - Panathenaic Procession (in the frieze)
  - Metopes:
    - Scenes on the Metopes were a symbolic representation of good vs evil, civilization vs. barbarism.
    - West: Greeks and Amazons
      - Mythical king of Athens marries the queen of the Amazons and they want their queen back.
    - South: Lapiths and Centaurs
      - Symbolizing civilized vs animal/savage behavior
    - East: Gods and Titans (Titanomachy: Ti tan-ah-ma-key)
      - War between the old gods and the Olympian gods
    - North: Greeks and Trojans
      - War started after the Trojan prince stole the wife of Menalaus. (meh·nuh·lay·uhs)
  - Rebuilding of the Parthenon on the site of the one damaged by the Persian War was somewhat symbolic; a rebirth of the former temple.
- The east pediment represented the birth of Athena. According to Greek mythology Zeus gave birth to Athena after a terrible headache prompted him to summon Hephaestus’ (the god of fire and the forge) assistance. To alleviate the pain he ordered Hephaestus to strike him with his forging hammer, and when he did, Zeus’ head split open and out popped the goddess Athena in full armor. The sculptural arrangement depicts the moment of Athena’s.
- Each side of the frieze contains a different narrative, three sides depicting battle and the east side a gathering of gods around Zeus, who is atop his throne
- The images of the parapet were not continuous narratives, but individual scenes of Athena Nike
- These images celebrate victory of Athens over enemies, other Greeks or Persians
- While it is not possible to know with certainty, it is likely that these young women were making their way to the festival to present a new peplos to the statue of Athena housed in the Erechtheion.

## Context

- The term acropolis is generic and there are many other acropolises in Greece
- During the first Persian war in 492–490 BCE, Athens played a central role in the defeat of the Persians.
- The Persians returned 10 years later with particular attention paid to Athens and the Acropolis.
  - They looted and burned buildings and destroyed many of the sculptures and pots there.
- Many objects from the Acropolis have found their way to museums in other parts of the world, sparking the discussion as to who actually owns these objects and should they be returned to the country or origin.
- Parthenon
- Dedicated to the Greek goddess Athena
  - Athena was known as the virgin goddess, thus the build was named for the Greek word for virgin, “Parthenon”
  - Athena Parthenos
- It is believed that the great sculptor Phidias oversaw all the sculpture for the structure
- Marble for this temple was quarried 10.5 miles away.
- Built during the high classical period of the 5th century BCE.
- When the Ottomans were in control of Greece during the 17th century, the Venetians who were at war with the Ottomans, attacked the Parthenon which was being used as an armory. The gunpowder exploded and destroyed the interior of the structure.
- In the 18th century, Lord Elgin was given permission from the Turkish government to remove sculptures that had fallen from the building. Most of them made their way to England.
- The design of this temple has influenced architectural design throughout western society.
  - Washington D.C. is a prime example, as many of our government buildings reference the Parthenon.
  - Athens was birthplace to democracy (albeit limited)
    - a system of government by the whole population or all the eligible members of a state, typically through elected representatives.
    - While the term democracy is often used synonymously with republic, there is a distinction,
    - By definition, a republic is a representative form of government that is ruled according to a charter, or constitution, and a democracy is a government that is ruled according to the will of the majority. Although these forms of government are often confused, they are quite different. The main difference between a republic and a democracy is the charter or constitution that limits power in a republic, often to protect the individual's rights against the desires of the majority.
- Helios, horses, and Dionysus (Heracles?)
- See context under acropolis
- Temple of Athena Nike
- Built between 426 - 421 BCE during the high classical period of Greece
- The architect was Kallikrates
- This temple was built to replace an earlier one that was destroyed during the Persian Wars.
- Was built on a bastion, that is, a projecting part of a fortification built at an angle to the line of a wall, so as to allow defensive fire in several directions.
- Around 410 BCE, a parapet was added around the edge of the cliff, to protect visitors.
  - It also had decorative relief carvings
- In 1687 the Ottomans who were in control of the acropolis at the time, dismantled the temple and parapet to reinforce the west side against Venetian attacks.
  - It has been rebuilt 3 times since then - 1835-36; 1940; and 2010
- Victory adjusting her sandal
- Around 410 BCE, a parapet was added around the edge of the cliff, to protect visitors.
  - It also had decorative relief carvings
