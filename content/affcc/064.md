# 64 — Golden Haggadah (The Plagues of Egypt, Scenes of Liberation, Preparation for Passover)

## Attribution

**Culture / Artist:** Late medieval Spain. 
**Date:** c. 1320 CE
**Medium:** Illuminated manuscript (pigments and gold leaf on vellum)
**Location:** Catalonia (Barcelona), Spain

## Form

- Hand painted and hand written
- Book form on vellum pages
- The Hebrew text, which reads from right to left
- Gold leaf backgrounds with decorative patterns
- Use of registers, with image pages broken into quadrants
  - Registers have colorful and highly decorative borders

## Function

- medieval illuminated manuscript, a daily book of prayer
- Serves a didactic purpose, teaching the story of the Jews’ deliverance from Egyptian bondage and slavery
- Its materials and opulence display the wealth of the book’s owners

## Content

- 56 images containing stories, most of which come from the book of Exodus
- The story of Passover—of miraculous salvation from slavery—is one that is recounted annually by many Jews at a seder, the ritual meal that marks the beginning of the holiday.
- Prayers and readings

## Context

- Haggadah means narration, telling
- Specifically made for use in the home
- Used during a special ritual meal called a seder
- This haggadah manuscript was copied and illuminated in Catalonia, north east Spain in approximately 1320 CE.
- The Golden Haggadah, as you might imagine given its name, is one of the most luxurious examples of these books ever created
